## Inflation continues to ease amid lower fuel prices in July

- Production strengthened further in July: The overall Industrial Production Index (IIP) expanded further, surging 14.5% YoY, bringing total IIP to increase 11.4% YoY, supported by the manufacturing sub-sector, which surged 12.0% YoY. According to the latest PMI survey, stronger new orders, particularly export orders, point to sustained export and manufacturing momentum in the coming months.

- July retail sales remained robust, although higher prices continued to drive headline retail sales growth: Total retail sales increased 13.1% YoY in 7M 2026 (7M 2025: +9.5% YoY). However, real retail sales grew 7.5% YoY, inching up from 7.4% YoY a year earlier. We expect domestic consumption to improve in the coming months, supported by stronger tourism, seasonal demand, higher public-sector incomes, and improving employment.

- State revenue completed 73% of annual plan in 7M 2026: The State budget recorded a surplus of USD17.9bn (+81% YoY), with revenue reaching 73% of the annual plan. State spending for investment and development surged 23.7% YoY to USD15.9bn, completing 37.4% of the annual plan (vs an average of 33.2% in 7M 2018- 2025). Despite implementation bottlenecks, the Government is piloting a KPI-based system to accelerate public investment disbursement, which remains a key driver of economic growth.

- FDI inflows remained robust in July and 7M 2026: In July, FDI registration jumped 32.3% YoY to USD3.4bn. In 7M 2026, total FDI registrations surged 58.0% to USD38.1bn. Meanwhile, FDI disbursements increased 15.4% YoY to USD2.2bn in July, resulting in a disbursement of USD15.2bn (+11.8% YoY) in 7M 2026. We expect both registered and disbursed FDI to remain strong, supported by ongoing investment commitments and project implementation.

- Trade remained robust, but the trade deficit widened further: In 7M 2026, both exports (USD319.5bn; +21.7% YoY) and imports (USD340.1bn; +34.8% YoY) maintained robust growth, leading to a cumulative trade deficit of USD20.5bn. The widening trade deficit was driven primarily by a surge in imports of PCs and electronics and petroleum products. According to the latest PMI, new export orders rose for a third straight month, signaling stronger exports during the August– November peak season, which could help narrow the record-high trade deficit.

- July’s CPI eased for the second consecutive month: July’s CPI rose 4.45% YoY (- 0.12% MoM) and 4.39% YoY in 7M 2026, mainly due to the decline in domestic gasoline and gas prices. Inflationary pressures could pick up in August, driven by (1) stronger end-of-summer holiday demand, (2) university tuition hikes for the 2026– 27 academic year, and (3) domestic gasoline prices averaging 8.6% above July’s levels, which could add around 0.3 ppt to headline CPI if prices remain unchanged.

- The VND appreciated against the USD in July: The USD/VND exchange rate remained stable in July, trading within a narrow range of 26,254–26,335. The VND appreciated 0.1% against the USD, closing the month at 26,296 in the interbank market and remaining broadly unchanged from end-2025. The stability was supported by a weaker DXY (-1.3%), VND appreciation in the black market (+1.7%), and resilient FDI inflows. While the record-high trade deficit could add pressure on the exchange rate, solid FDI disbursement growth (+11.8% YoY in 7M 2026) and a stable black-market exchange rate should help mitigate risks.

## Industrial Production

Production strengthened further in July

Following strong growth in June, the overall Industrial Production Index (IIP) expanded further in July, surging 14.5% YoY (July 2025: +7.5% YoY) – well above the average monthly growth of 9.9% in H1 2026 – supported mainly by robust growth of the manufacturing sub-sector at 15.0% YoY (July 2025: +7.6% YoY). This brings total IIP growth in 7M 2026 to 11.4% YoY (7M 2025: +8.4% YoY), driven primarily by the manufacturing sub-sector, which surged 12.0%.

In 7M 2026, all manufacturing sub-sectors continued to post growth, with around two-thirds achieving double-digit expansion. Basic metals (+23.5% YoY), repair, maintenance, and installation of machinery and equipment (+22.4% YoY), motor vehicles (+16.0% YoY), beverages (+15.7% YoY), and other non-metallic mineral products (+13.5% YoY) were the top performers.

Vietnam's manufacturing improves as output and new orders rise while

inflationary pressures ease The S&P Global Vietnam Manufacturing PMI rose to 52.9 in July from 51.8 in June, signaling stronger growth momentum in Vietnam's manufacturing sector.

Output growth accelerated in July to the highest level in five months, extending improvement in manufacturing activity since May 2025 while new orders increased for the third consecutive month. Notably, total new orders were supported by a strong rise in new export orders, with the index increasing at the fastest pace since July 2024. Meanwhile, other aspects also improved, including firms continuing to increase purchasing activity and employment rising for the first time in five months while input costs and output prices both rose at the slowest pace since September 2025.

Outlook: According to the latest PMI survey, the sharp increase in total new orders, particularly export orders, points to stronger export momentum in the coming months, which should help sustain robust domestic production.

## Consumption

July retail sales remained robust, although higher prices remained the main driver of headline retail sales growth

After accelerating in May and June, total retail sales of goods and services remained robust in July, surging 14.5% YoY (July 2025: +9.9% YoY) – the highest monthly growth rate since February 2023. In 7M 2026, total retail sales increased by 13.1% YoY in nominal terms, well above 9.5% YoY growth recorded in 7M 2025. However, excluding price effect, real retail sales only grew 7.5% YoY, inching up from 7.4% YoY a year earlier – indicating that higher prices, rather than stronger

consumer demand, were the main driver of headline growth. In 7M 2026, international tourism remained one of the key drivers, with retail sales of accommodation & catering services and tourism services growing strongly by 15.9% YoY and 16.3% YoY, respectively. In addition, international arrivals continued to increase 6.6% YoY to 1.7 million in July, bringing the 7M total to 13.9 million (+13.8% YoY), or 56% of the annual target.

Meanwhile, retail sales of goods (accounting for 75.5% of total retail sales) continued to grow solidly at 12.7% YoY, mainly supported by strong growth in retail sales of gemstones, precious metals, & products (+28.2% YoY), petroleum products (+15.2% YoY), wood & construction materials (+15.2% YoY), and other fuels, excluding gasoline and oil (+15.1% YoY).

## State Budget

State revenue completed 73% of annual plan in 7M 2026

According to the Ministry of Finance (MoF), State budget revenue increased 16.0% YoY to VND1,834.6tn (USD69.8bn) in 7M 2026, completing 72.5% of the full-year target. Of the total, domestic revenue increased 16.0% YoY to VND1,587.5tn (USD60.4bn). Meanwhile, revenue from export–import activities and crude oil revenue increased 13.7% YoY and 29.6% YoY to VND208.8tn (USD7.9bn) and VND37.3tn (USD1.4bn), respectively.

On the expenditure side, State spending grew 7.7% YoY to VND1,364.3tn (USD51.9bn) in 7M 2026, fulfilling 43.2% of the full-year target. Of this total, current expenditure edged up 1.4% YoY to VND875.0tn (USD33.3bn), achieving 48.4% of the full-year target. In addition, public investment disbursement remained solid, with total disbursement in 7M 2026 surging 23.7% YoY to VND418.9tn (USD15.9bn) and completing 37.4% of the annual plan (compared to the 7M 2018- 2025 average of 33.2%).

As a result, the State budget recorded a surplus of VND470.3tn (USD17.9bn) in 7M 2026, significantly higher than the VND260.1tn (USD9.9bn) surplus recorded in 7M 2025. Outlook: While land clearance delays, material shortages, and rising construction material costs continue to pose challenges, the Government has introduced a pilot KPI-based system to monitor public investment disbursement, identify implementation bottlenecks, and expedite project execution through the remainder of the year. Accelerating public investment disbursement remains a key policy priority to sustain economic growth in the period ahead.

## FDI

FDI inflows remained robust in July and 7M 2026

In July, FDI registration continued to grow strongly, surging 32.3% YoY to USD3.4bn, bringing total FDI registrations in 7M 2026 to USD38.1bn (+58.0% YoY) – roughly equivalent to the total for 2025 (USD38.4bn). Meanwhile, FDI disbursements remained strong, surging 15.4% YoY to USD2.2bn in July and increasing 11.8% YoY to USD15.2bn in 7M 2026. Of total FDI registrations in 7M 2026, the manufacturing & processing sector (USD18.7bn) continued to lead, accounting for 49.1% of total FDI pledges. The real estate sector ranked second with USD5.7bn (14.9% share).

In terms of registration by country/region, Singapore (USD10.4bn; 27.3% share) ranked first. South Korea (USD8.2bn; 21.6% share) and Hong Kong (USD4.9bn; 12.9%) followed to rank second and third among the top foreign investors in Vietnam in 7M 2026.

Outlook: We expect both registered and disbursed FDI to remain solid in the coming months, supported by ongoing investment commitments from multinational manufacturers and continued project implementation. Nevertheless, uncertainty surrounding US Section 301 investigations related to IP rights and structural excess capacity remain key risks to monitor toward the year-end.

## Trade

Trade remained robust, however the trade deficit expanded further

According to NSO data, July marked another record month for both exports and imports, with exports increasing 25.0% YoY to USD53.1bn while imports outpaced exports, surging 41.4% YoY to USD56.7bn, marking a continued trade deficit of USD3.6bn. In 7M 2026, exports and imports continued to grow impressively, reaching USD319.5bn (+21.7% YoY) and USD340.1bn (+34.8% YoY), respectively, bringing the cumulative trade deficit to a record USD20.5bn. The widening trade deficit was driven primarily by a surge in imports of PCs and electronics (trade deficit:

USD50.6bn in 7M 2026, +2.0x YoY), alongside higher imports of petroleum products (deficit: USD6.1bn in 7M 2026, up 1.7x YoY). Overall, we view the widening trade deficit as continuing to reflect strong imports of production inputs rather than weakening export demand.

Breaking down the 7M 2026 performance, the top three export categories posted strong growth: PCs & electronics (USD85.2bn; +50.1% YoY, accounting for 26.7% of total exports), machinery & equipment (USD40.2bn; +24.6% YoY, 12.6% share), and phones & spare parts (USD38.5bn; +18.6% YoY, 12.0% share). On the import side, the top three categories were PCs & electronics (USD135.8bn; +65.9% YoY), machinery & equipment (USD41.1bn; +22.7% YoY), and fabrics (USD8.9bn; +2.2% YoY).

The US remained Vietnam’s largest export destination with exports to the US continuing to surge around 28% YoY in July (following +32.6% YoY in January, +9.7% YoY in February, +28.4% YoY in March, +25.6% YoY in Apr, +12.9% YoY in May, and +23.1% YoY in June), bringing total 7M 2026 exports to the US to USD104.7bn (+23.0% YoY). China ranked second (USD45.6bn; +30.7% YoY) while the EU ranked third (USD37.9bn; +17.9% YoY). Meanwhile, imports from China – Vietnam’s largest import supplier – surged 36.6% YoY to USD138.6bn, while imports from South Korea – Vietnam’s second largest import partner – also jumped 52.4% YoY to USD51.4bn.

## Inflation

July’s CPI eased for the second consecutive month

In July, the Consumer Price Index (CPI) declined 0.12% MoM and increased 4.45% YoY, bringing average CPI to 4.39% in 7M 2026. July CPI eased mainly due to the decline in domestic gasoline and gas prices. The key factors affecting CPI in July include:

- The CPI of the transportation category declined 2.02% MoM, curbing headline CPI by around 0.2 ppt, mainly due to a 4.35% decline in domestic gasoline prices (curbing headline CPI by around 0.15 ppt).

- The CPI of the housing and construction materials category declined 0.07%, curbing headline CPI by 0.02 ppt, mainly due to a 11.4% decline in domestic gas prices (curbing headline CPI by around 0.17 ppt) following a decline in global gas prices, offsetting the increase in house rental prices (+0.41%) and electricity prices (+1.55%). Outlook: Several factors could add to inflationary pressure in August, including (1) higher demand during the end-of-summer holiday period; (2) tuition hikes at many universities starting in August for the 2026–2027 academic year; and (3) the current average domestic gasoline prices in August are 8.6% higher than the average in July, which could lift headline CPI by around 0.3 ppt if prices remain unchanged during the month.

## Foreign Exchange

The VND appreciated against the USD in July

The USD/VND exchange rate continued to remain stable throughout July, trading within a range of 26,254–26,335. The VND appreciated around 0.1% against the USD in July and closed the month at 26,296 in the interbank market (the USD/VND was nearly unchanged vs end-2025). The stability of the USD/VND exchange rate in July was supported mainly by the decline of the DXY (- 1.3%), the VND appreciating around 1.7% against the USD in the black market, and ongoing solid FDI inflows.

Outlook: The ongoing record-high trade deficit could weigh on the USD/VND exchange rate if sustained. However, we expect that the solid FDI inflows (7M 2026 disbursements up 11.8% YoY) and the stable black-market exchange rate could help to ease some pressure on the FX rate.
