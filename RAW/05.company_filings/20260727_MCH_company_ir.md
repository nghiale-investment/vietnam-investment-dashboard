# Executive Summary

- Masan Consumer (MCH) delivered volume-led 2Q2026 revenue growth of 14.2% YoY to VND7,165 billion. 1H2026 revenue rose 13.6% to VND15,637 billion, within the company's 11-15% FY2026 guidance range.
- 2Q gross profit increased 15.3% to VND3,193 billion and gross margin expanded 44 bps YoY to 44.6%. Selective pricing in Seasonings, Convenience Foods, and home and personal care (HPC) offset raw-material inflation and product-upgrade costs.
- 2Q NPAT Pre-MI rose 10.2% to VND1,484 billion, representing a 20.7% net margin. Earnings growth lagged revenue as logistics costs rose to 4.4% of revenue from 3.2% because of higher freight and fixed distribution costs, partly offset by network optimization.
- Growth was broad-based: Seasonings rose 17.1%, Bottled Beverages 17.8%, HPC 32.5%, and Global Business 24.0% in 2Q. Convenience Foods grew 7.4%, supported by Omachi premiumization, while Coffee grew 0.9%.
- Retail Supreme continued to deepen general-trade execution. Active selling outlets reached approximately 550,000, SKUs per outlet reached 5.8, and about 43,000 outlets sold more than six categories. Management is shifting from expanding coverage toward improving productivity per outlet.
- MCH generated approximately VND2,000 billion of free cash flow in 1H2026. The company highlighted cumulative cash dividends of approximately VND32 trillion over FY2023-FY2025 and its addition to the VN30 Index at the July 2026 review, effective from the early-August rebalance.

# Segment Commentary

## Retail Engine

- General Trade revenue grew 10.6% YoY in 2Q, extending the recovery established in 1Q as Retail Supreme execution improved.
- Active selling outlets reached approximately 550,000. SKUs per outlet rose to approximately 5.8 against a year-end target of 7.0, while about 43,000 outlets sold more than six categories against a year-end target of 100,000.
- Management is shifting from breadth to depth: streamlining sales-force operations, improving in-store execution and display, and directing innovation toward the 30,000 highest-traffic outlets. The objective is to raise revenue per outlet and accelerate underpenetrated categories.
- Modern Trade and HORECA revenue grew 28.9% and 29.7%, respectively. E-commerce grew 202.3% from a low base.

## Brand Engine

- MCH described three growth levers: premiumization, market-share gains, and new growth engines.
- CHIN-SU revenue rose 27.0% on double-digit volume growth and drove premiumization in Seasonings.
- Omachi revenue rose 17.8% on mid-single-digit volume growth and generated most incremental Convenience Foods revenue. Its value share increased 1.3 percentage points in 1H2026.
- Nam Ngu regained share lost during the 2025 general-trade disruption, with high-single-digit fish-sauce volume growth. Kokomi gained 0.3 percentage points of value share despite contraction in mass-market noodles.
- Bottled Beverages returned to 17.8% growth on high-single-digit volume growth. Wake-Up 247 remained the core volume driver, while newer products contributed incremental demand.
- Management identified two 2H catalysts: a health-and-wellness innovation pipeline and Retail Supreme as a targeted on-premise activation platform for new launches.

## New Growth Engines

- HPC grew 32.5% in 2Q, primarily from volume. Chante, roughly half of the HPC portfolio, drove most of the increase. Homey, approximately 10% of the portfolio, scaled from near zero to about VND20 billion of monthly revenue as Retail Supreme deepened distribution.
- Chante Active launched in June 2026 to broaden the consumer base and support further general-trade share gains.
- Global Business grew 24.0% across ASEAN, the EU, the United States, and Japan.

## Digital Engine

- Digital tools are being used to improve sales-force productivity, target trade programs to individual retailers, and deploy the appropriate brands and promotions to each outlet.
- MCH is optimizing retailer-membership programs to reinforce loyalty and increase assortment per outlet.

## 1H Outlook

- The source reported completed 1H2026 results and maintained the FY2026 revenue-growth outlook of 11-15%.
- Demand was strong in April, softened in May, and recovered toward normal run rates in June. Management attributed below-trend 2Q growth to normal intra-period variability rather than a structural demand change.
- Sequential gross-margin moderation versus 1Q was concentrated in Seasonings and HPC. Seasonings experienced the normal post-Tet mix reversal, while HPC absorbed higher raw-material costs. Other category margins were broadly stable QoQ.
- The health-and-wellness pipeline, Retail Supreme activations, greater outlet productivity, and continued premiumization are the main company-identified 2H growth drivers.

## 2026 Budget

- MCH reaffirmed FY2026 revenue guidance of VND33,800-35,000 billion, equivalent to 11-15% YoY growth.

# Investment View

## Positives

- Growth was predominantly volume-led and broad across premium brands, beverages, HPC, Global Business, and faster-growing channels, supporting the quality of the top-line expansion.
- Retail Supreme is moving from distribution repair toward outlet productivity, with clear KPIs for active outlets, assortment depth, and priority locations.
- Gross-margin expansion despite raw-material and product-upgrade costs indicates some pricing and mix resilience.
- The net-cash balance sheet, approximately VND2,000 billion of 1H free cash flow, and recent dividend history support MCH's capacity for shareholder returns.
- VN30 inclusion may improve index-linked demand and trading liquidity.

## Areas to Monitor

- 2Q earnings growth trailed revenue growth because logistics costs rose 120 bps of revenue. The durability of network optimization benefits is therefore important.
- Two-year reported revenue CAGR was only about 6%, or approximately 8% after the company's destocking adjustment, leaving medium-term growth dependent on successful distribution and category expansion.
- Convenience Foods grew more slowly than major growth categories, and Coffee was nearly flat in 2Q.
- The large increase in outstanding shares affects per-share comparisons; management's like-for-like EPS adjustment should be used when assessing underlying growth.
- The cash balance declined despite strong reported free cash flow, consistent with the company's dividend model but reducing the net-cash buffer.

# Catalysts

- Continued double-digit volume growth and delivery near the high end of FY2026 revenue, EBITDA, and NPAT Pre-MI guidance.
- Retail Supreme raising SKUs and revenue per outlet, particularly across the 30,000 highest-traffic locations.
- Health-and-wellness product launches in 2H2026 and targeted Retail Supreme activation.
- Sustained premiumization and share gains for CHIN-SU, Omachi, Nam Ngu, and Kokomi.
- Further scaling of HPC, Global Business, modern trade, HORECA, and e-commerce.
- VN30 inclusion and associated index-linked liquidity.
- Continued high cash generation supporting dividends.

# Risks

- Consumer demand may remain uneven, as shown by the April-May-June volatility in 2Q.
- Higher freight, fixed distribution, raw-material, and product-upgrade costs could continue to compress net-margin growth relative to revenue.
- Retail Supreme may fall short of year-end assortment and multi-category outlet targets.
- Premiumization, health-and-wellness launches, and newer HPC brands may not scale as expected.
- Convenience Foods or Coffee weakness could offset growth in newer categories.
- High dividend distributions could reduce balance-sheet flexibility if free cash flow weakens.
- Rights issues, bonus shares, and ESOP issuance can dilute reported per-share growth and complicate historical comparability.
