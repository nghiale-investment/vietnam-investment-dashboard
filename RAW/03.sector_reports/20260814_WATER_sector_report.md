 Water tariff hike delay overshadows robust water demand
 We broadly maintain our 2026-2030F water volume growth forecasts for BWE and TDM.

 BWE’s H1 2026 volume was 111.1 million cbm (+7.9% YoY), broadly in line with our full-year growth
 forecast of 8%. TDM’s 7M 2026 volume was 42.4 million cbm (+13.6% YoY) vs our full-year
 forecast of 15%. Even though uncertainties from the Middle East conflict might weigh on
 consumption from industrial clients to some extent, we assume El Nino conditions (hotter
 weather than the average) will support strong water demand, especially in H2 2026 and 2027. As
 a result, we maintain our respective 2026 and 2027 volume growth assumptions of 8% and 11%
 for BWE and volume growth assumptions of 15%/14% for TDM.
 In this Update Report, we delay the 3% water tariff hike for BWE by one year from 2027F to
 2028F, reflecting a longer-than-expected regulatory review process. We expect the

 potential passage of the Law on Water Supply and Drainage in 2027 and following
 Resolutions/Circulars, to provide a clearer legal and cost-based framework for future tariff
 adjustments.
 We lower our target price (TP) for BWE by 3.9% to VND49,600/share and downgrade our
 rating from BUY to OUTPERFORM. Our lower TP is mainly due to (1) a 9% downward revision in
 our aggregate NPAT-MI forecast for 2026-2030F, with respective changes of -5%/-15%/-9%/-
 9%/-7% for 2026/27/28/29/30F and (2) a 1 ppt increase in our risk-free rate from 6.0% to 7.0%,
 which more than offset (3) our incorporation of the newly acquired Phu Yen Water (PWS).

 BWE’s valuation looks relatively attractive at 2026/27F P/Es of 13.2x/9.8x, implying a PEG of 0.4
 and 0.3, respectively, based on a 2026-2028 EPS CAGR of 34%.

 (37% stake), (2) our 25% lower valuation for TDM’s standalone water business due to higher net

 by 1 ppt, which outweigh (4) the contribution from newly acquired PWS.

 We view TDM’s valuation as fair, with 2026 parent and consolidated P/Es of 35.7x and 14.3x,

                     Industry Update
                     H1 2026 GRDP shows strong YoY growth, robust FDI inflows

                     Ho Chi Minh City has officially set a 2026 GRDP target of over 10% for 2026. To achieve this
                     ambitious goal, the city continues focusing on the three traditional growth drivers: production,
                     consumption, and exports. In addition, it will focus on developing three new drivers: an
                     international financial center, a port and logistics system, and innovations such as green
                     transformation and digital transformation.

                     Public investment has been reaffirmed as a primary strategic pillar, with the region's
                     connectivity poised for a historic leap as several strategic infrastructure projects enter active
                     phases. The city has moved to fast-track the Ben Thanh–Can Gio 350 km/h high-speed rail,
                     alongside the Can Gio Bridge and Phu My 2 Bridge. Furthermore, 2026 is projected to be a
                     landmark year for urban transit, with the scheduled groundbreaking of four major metro lines:
                     Metro Line 2, Metro Line 6, the Thu Thiem–Long Thanh link, and a critical line connecting to the
                     former Binh Duong Province. These transit projects, combined with the operational integration
                     of Ring Road 3 and National Route 13, are expected to unlock significant land value and industrial
                     capacity.

                     Effective July 1, 2025, Binh Duong Province was merged with Ba Ria–Vung Tau Province and
                     Ho Chi Minh City. Following the merger, the new administrative entity retains the name Ho Chi
                     Minh City and all economic indicators will be consolidated and reported for this new HCMC. Until
                     now, HCMC has remained under the water tariff mechanism that was in place prior to the merger.

                     HCMC’s H1 2026 GRDP growth was 8.55% YoY, the highest rise over the last ten years. The
                     GRDP of the industrial-construction, agricultural-forestry-fishery, and services sectors
                     increased 8.50%, 2.99%, and 8.89% YoY, respectively. Q2 2026 witnessed robust GRDP growth
                     of 8.53% YoY, following Q1 growth of 8.57%.
                     As of H1 2026, total investment disbursement was VND310tn (+9.9% YoY), in which public
                     disbursement was VND74tn (+7.7% YoY; 23.8% of total disbursement).

                     HCMC continues to affirm its leading position as Vietnam’s top destination for FDI, with
                     cumulative FDI reaching USD153bn by the end of H1 2026 - accounting for 27.2% of the national
                     total. HCMC’s FDI surpasses that of the next three cities/provinces - Bac Ninh (former Bac Ninh,
                     Bac Giang), Hai Phong (former Hai Phong, Hai Duong), and Dong Nai (former Dong Nai, Binh
                     Phuoc) - combined.

                     In 6M 2026, HCMC secured USD7.9bn in registered FDI (2.1x YoY) with 1,044 additional new
                     projects and 150 projects adjusted to increase capital. This result completes 72% of the city’s
                     FDI attraction target of USD11bn in 2026.
                     Former Binh Duong is among Vietnam’s top provinces in attracting new FDI registrations,
                     which drives robust water demand from industrial parks. As of the end of Q2 2025, cumulative
                     FDI for the province over the past 30 years was more than USD42.4bn, ranking third in Vietnam,
                     trailing only former HCMC and Hanoi. A recent notable FDI project in Binh Duong is the large-scale
                     investment project from LEGO (total capital of USD1.3bn).

                     Appendix 2: Water pricing mechanism

                     According to Decree 117/2007/ND-CP, issued on July 11, 2007, water rates for end-users in a
                     province must be approved or determined by provincial authorities. These tariffs also need to fall
                     within the price range that the Ministry of Finance has set (Figure 47). Wholesale and retail water
                     supply firms should come to an agreement on wholesale clean water prices. In the event of a
                     dispute, either party (or both parties) may request the intervention of provincial authorities.
                     Additionally, Circular 44/2021/TT-BTC, dated June 18, 2021, which outlines the guiding principles,
                     procedures, and legal frameworks for establishing clean water rates, was put into effect by the
                     Ministry of Finance.

                     Figure 47: Regulated tariff range for clean water, including VAT

                      VND/cbm                                Minimum Maximum
                      Special municipalities and Tier 1 cities  3,500  18,000
                      Tier 2, 3, 4, & 5 cities                  3,000  15,000
                      Rural areas                               2,000  11,000

                     Source: Circular 44/2021/TT-BTC, Vietcap
                     We understand that the water tariff is set based on the unit cost of producing clean water

                     plus a predetermined return, as below (Circular 44/2021):
                     Cost per cbm =
                     Material cost + Labor cost + Depreciation cost + SG&A expense + Financial expense + Safety fee - Non water revenue
                                           Total sales volume (*)
                     In which:
                     (*): Total production volume minus water loss. According to the circular, the maximum water loss
                     ratio of 20% shall be included in the water tariff calculation. In 2025, the maximum water ratio of
                     15% shall be included in water tariff calculations unless otherwise determined by the Prime
                     Minister.

                     The water tariff is regulated by the formula below:
                                   Water tariff (VND per cbm) = (Cost per cbm + P) * Hi

                          P: Regulated profit, which is regulated to range from VND360 to VND1,500 per cbm.
                          Hi: Coefficient, which is subject to approval by provincial authorities. Hi = 1 for wholesale
                          clients.

                     We expect that water tariffs throughout Vietnam will continue to increase moving forward.
