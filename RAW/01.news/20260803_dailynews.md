# Daily News - 2026-08-03

## News Summary

The August 3 capture pointed to improving domestic manufacturing momentum, abundant short-term liquidity, lower oil prices, stronger July activity data, and a broad rebound in Vietnam equities. SHS said Vietnam's July 2026 manufacturing PMI rose to 52.9 from 51.8 in June, the highest in five months, with output, new orders, export orders and purchasing activity all improving; it was the thirteenth consecutive month of better business conditions. SSI said Brent fell more than 4% to around USD84/bbl after President Donald Trump suspended a planned strike on Iran and resumed peace talks, while OPEC+ approved another modest production-quota increase. SSI also said the SBV injected a net VND12.3tn during 27-31 July and the overnight interbank rate fell to 0.75%, the lowest since March 2024, although longer-tenor rates remained elevated because of the credit-deposit gap. SSI and SHS both flagged that the SBV raised the deductible share of State Treasury term deposits in LDR calculations from 20% to 50%, a change mainly benefiting state-owned banks.

SSI's later market outlook said global conditions were still volatile because chip-led AI trades had cooled, but it also framed Vietnam as a possible beneficiary of capital rotation from AI-concentrated markets into traditional markets, using Singapore's bank-led STI rally as a comparison. Locally, SSI said last week's broad-based rally suggested sentiment was turning more constructive after investors increasingly viewed the market as having formed a base. SSI's mid-day note said the VNIndex extended the rebound by another 1.16% to 1,755.95 despite low turnover of VND8.7tn, with gains led by financials, mid-cap real estate, steel and technology; foreigners net bought VND564bn in the morning, led by FPT, HPG and FRT.

The July macro data were stronger on production and external activity but still showed inflation and funding pressure. SSI said July delivered sequential improvement versus June across industrial output, retail sales, trade volumes and public investment, with IIP up 14.5% YoY and 1.2% MoM, seven-month IIP up 11.4%, manufacturing up 12.0%, metals up 23.5%, and electronics up 12.8%. SHS separately summarized GSO data showing July CPI up 4.45% YoY, seven-month CPI up 4.39%, core inflation up 4.19%, July retail sales up 14.5% YoY, seven-month retail sales up 13.1% nominal and 7.5% real, July exports up 25% YoY, imports up 41.4% YoY, seven-month exports up 21.7% YoY, imports up 34.8% YoY, a USD20.52bn trade deficit, public investment disbursement of VND445.5tn equal to 39% of plan, realized FDI of USD15.2bn, newly registered FDI above USD21.05bn, and 13.9mn international arrivals in the first seven months. SHS also noted the SBV central rate at VND25,358/USD, interbank USD/VND at 26,291, free-market USD/VND at 26,270, and economy-wide credit of nearly VND20.1 quadrillion as of July 13, up 7.86% YTD. SHS interpreted FX conditions as stable, headline inflation as cooling while core pressure persisted, production and consumption as improving, and fast credit growth as a reason to keep watching deposit mobilization and liquidity.

Corporate updates focused on PLX, NLG and HDG. SHS said PLX Q2 2026 pretax profit reached VND3.364tn, bringing first-half pretax profit to VND2.868tn, up 43% YoY and completing about 84% of the full-year plan, supported by above-plan sales volume, inventory provision reversals and positive contributions from subsidiaries. NLG first-half presales exceeded VND5tn, completing about 73% of the annual plan, with Mizuki Park and Sol Garden contributing nearly 45% of total presales; buyer advances of nearly VND3tn created a base for revenue and profit recognition in coming quarters. HDG Q2 revenue fell 18% YoY to VND486bn, while consolidated NPAT nearly quadrupled YoY to about VND104bn; first-half revenue was VND1.17tn, down 2%, and net profit was VND100bn, down 27%, due to VND879bn of provisions, including more than VND800bn related to the Hong Phong 4 project.

Vietcap said PNJ rose 7% without a specific company catalyst and was among the top five foreign net buys of the day, but it also reminded readers that PNJ's late-week results were weak: first-half 2026 net revenue rose 49% YoY and NPAT rose 6% YoY, completing 56% and 38% of Vietcap's respective 2026 forecasts and trailing expectations, while Q2 recorded a VND283bn net loss after VND865bn of provision expense related to July buyback activities. HSC posted an invite to its August 3 webinar, "The Valuation Disconnect," at 15:30 Vietnam time.
