ETF Update - Week 03-07 Aug: Foreign investors returned to net buying after weeks of selling

 

Vietnam major ETFs returned to net outflows of USD2.2mn, reversing from a USD3.1mn net inflow, mainly driven by renewed redemptions from the Fubon FTSE Vietnam ETF.
Foreign investors turned net buyers, recording VND2.25tn (c. USD86mn) of net buying versus VND2.43tn (c. USD93mn) of net selling previously, led by FPT, DMX, and VIC on the buying side, while VPB, TCB, and NLG saw the strongest foreign selling.
ASEAN ETF flows remained mixed, with Singapore leading regional inflows, while Thailand and Vietnam recorded the largest outflows.
 

Banks - The market is circulating an internal SBV document outlining stricter measures for banks that exceed their assigned credit growth quotas in 2026. Under the document, banks breaching the overall credit growth limit would have the excess outstanding balance deducted from the following month’s lending ceiling, while breaches of the real-estate credit cap would trigger an additional penalty equivalent to 0.1% of the bank’s annual maximum credit balance per violation. Banks with more than three breaches may also become ineligible for further quota increases during the year. The mechanism would start to be applied from Sep-2026.

 

The document suggests that the SBV is continuing to closely control credit growth, broadly similar to its approach in 1Q26, but with an even tighter framework as credit growth would now be monitored and enforced on a monthly basis.
The key objective is likely to stabilize system liquidity and the interest-rate environment, while preventing an overly aggressive deposit-rate race in the final months of the year.
 

GMD - Key takeaways from the earnings call on 11 Aug:

 

7M26 volume update. GMD’s total throughput reached 3.05mn TEUs in 7M26, up 8% y/y, mainly driven by Gemalink. Gemalink’s throughput increased 24% y/y to 1.32mn TEUs, while Nam Dinh Vu handled 790,000 TEUs (down 10% y/y) and Phuoc Long handled 937,000 TEUs (up 6% y/y).
In July, GMD’s throughput reached 473,000 TEUs, up 7% y/y. Gemalink handled 222,000 TEUs (up 27% y/y and 17% m/m), while Nam Dinh Vu handled 120,000 TEUs (down 7% y/y but up 12% m/m), supported by a new service launched in late May 2026.
Investment projects. Gemalink Phase 2, with capex of USD220mn—85% financed by VND-denominated bank loans—is expected to commence operations in 3Q27–4Q27, adding 1.2mn TEUs of annual capacity. Gemalink Phase 3 (capacity 1mn TEUs/annum) is expected to begin construction in late 2028 and commence operations in late 2029. The new timeline of Phase 2 is on track, but the Phase 3 is slower than our expectation as previously, GMD planned to start construction of Phase 3 in FY26 and put into operation by late FY28.
Upon completion of all three phases, Gemalink’s design capacity will reach 4.0mn TEUs/annum from 1.8mn TEUs/annum currently, while effective capacity could reach 4.8–5.2mn TEUs. This should allow Gemalink to remain the largest port in the Cai Mep–Thi Vai area through 2030.
GMD is also seeking to invest in the Cai Mep Ha Logistics Centre and develop value-added maritime services. However, these initiatives remain at an early stage, with no clear earnings contribution at this point. We note that our model incorporated the Gemalink expansion projects but did not incorporate the Logistics center and value-added maritime services.
HSC’s comments. Gemalink and Phuoc Long’s 7M26 throughput remained broadly in line with our forecasts, completing 61% and 56% of our respective FY26 forecasts. In contrast, Nam Dinh Vu’s throughput was below our expectation, completing only 46% of our FY26 forecast.
Nevertheless, Nam Dinh Vu’s throughput should gradually improve, supported by the new service added in May, another expected in August, and potentially two to three additional services during the final four months of 2026 per management. We will continue to monitor the ramp-up of these services and update our forecasts once there is greater visibility on their volume contribution.
