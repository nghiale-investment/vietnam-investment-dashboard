HPG - Q2 core earnings extend gains on margin
expansion; in line with expectations - Earnings Flash
Company Research 01 Aug 2026
HPG announced its Q2 2026 results as follows:
- Net revenue: VND55.2tn (USD2.1bn; +4% QoQ, +54% YoY).
- NPAT-MI: VND6.4tn (USD240.4mn; -29% QoQ, +50% YoY).
- Core NPAT-MI: VND6.4tn (USD240.4mn; +28% QoQ, +50% YoY).
- Sales volume of key steel products:
+ Construction steel: ~1.3mn tonnes (-9% QoQ; +2% YoY).
+ HRC: ~1.93mn tonnes (+31% QoQ; +64% YoY; a new quarterly record).
H1 2026 results:
- Net revenue: VND108.1tn (USD4.1bn; +47% YoY; 52% of our 2026F forecast).
- NPAT-MI: VND15.4tn (USD579.8mn; +102% YoY; 59% of our 2026F forecast).
- Core NPAT-MI: VND11.4tn (USD428.5mn; +49% YoY; 52% of our 2026F forecast).
- Sales volume of key steel products:
+ Construction steel: ~2.74mn tonnes (+11% YoY; 49% of our 2026F forecast).
+ HRC: ~3.4mn tonnes (+57% YoY; 51% of our 2026F forecast).
Record HRC volume drove Q2 revenue growth, though construction steel softened QoQ:
Q2 2026 net revenue rose 4% QoQ and 54% YoY, as steel revenue increased 5% QoQ and 59%
YoY.
Q2 HRC sales volume reached a new quarterly record of ~1.93mn tonnes (+31% QoQ, +64%
YoY), which we attribute to (1) DQSC2 running with both Phase 1 and Phase 2 operational, vs
only Phase 1 in Q2 2025, (2) the old AD20 on narrow-width Chinese HRC and the new anticircumvention tariff on wide-width HRC (effective April 2026), which redirected demand
toward domestic supply, and (3) possible front-loaded stocking, as Q2 shipments were
booked when commodity prices remained elevated - in line with HPG’s practice of typically
selling HRC under forward contracts. H1 2026 HRC volume was ~3.4mn tonnes (+57% YoY; 51%
of our 2026F forecast), in line with our projection.

Q2 construction steel volume dropped 9% QoQ to ~1.30mn tonnes (+2%
YoY), attributed to buyers holding off on restocking amid expectations of softer steel prices
following the de-escalation of the Middle East conflict and a subsequent decline in commodity
prices. H1 2026 construction steel volume was ~2.73mn tonnes (+11% YoY; 49% of our 2026F
forecast), broadly in line with our projections. We believe underlying demand remains intact,
with the QoQ softness driven mainly by short-term factors rather than any structural change
in end-demand.
Q2 GPM expanded both QoQ and YoY, supported by favorable steel inventory timing and
favorable steel input-output dynamics:
Q2 2026 GPM was 19.0%, up from 15.8% in Q1 2026 and 18.4% in Q2 2025. The improvement
was led by the steel segment, with steel NPAT-MI margin expanding to 11.1% (from 8.8% in Q1
2026 and 10.3% in Q2 2025) - up both QoQ and YoY. On a QoQ basis, we attribute the margin
expansion to favorable inventory timing, as selling prices increased faster than the cost of
inventories procured before the Middle East-driven commodity price rally. This is consistent
with our expectations. We also note that HPG’s margin improvement in Q2 is consistent with
HSG’s (a large downstream flat-steel producer) recently reported results, with its core net
margin improving to 3.8% from 1.5% QoQ.
On a YoY basis, steel margin also expanded. Aside from the short-term inventory timing effect,
we think the margin expansion was also driven by structural factors, including Chinese steel
capacity cuts under its anti-involution policy and tighter export controls, alongside improving
iron ore supply - particularly from the ramp-up of the Simandou project - keeping input costs
favorable relative to output prices.
SG&A expenses rose sharply on higher selling expenses while G&A expenses remain soft:
Q2 SG&A expenses rose 17% QoQ and 76% YoY, equivalent to 3.7% of sales (vs 3.3% in Q1 2026
and 3.2% in Q2 2025). The increase was mainly driven by selling expenses (+28% QoQ, +108%
YoY) - mainly due to logistics costs, outsourcing and others - outweighing a decline in G&A
expenses (-20% QoQ, -4% YoY). The breakdown of these two accounts can be found in Figure
3.
We attribute the increase in selling expenses to (1) expansion in operations, with a larger
revenue scale, and (2) higher energy costs linked to the Middle East conflict (which raises the
SG&A/sales ratio, with H1 ratio of 3.5% - higher vs our full-year projection of 3.0%).
Financial expenses continued to rise on higher interest expenses; Q1’s one-off real estate gain
was not repeated in Q2:
Financial income fell 90% QoQ to VND617bn (USD23.3mn), as Q1 2026 included the one-off
VND4.9tn (USD185.5mn) pre-tax gain from HPG’s divestment of its stake in the Pho Noi Hung

Yen residential project; no similar one-off was recorded in Q2. Reported and core NPAT-MI are
therefore identical in Q2 2026. On a YoY basis, financial income rose 24% YoY, driven by higher
deposit interest income.
Financial expenses rose 3% QoQ and 97% YoY to VND1,926bn (USD72.7mn), driven by higher
interest expenses (+14% QoQ, +246% YoY). The YoY increase was driven by the cessation of
interest capitalization on DQSC2 Phase 2 following its commissioning in September 2025. The
QoQ increase was mainly due to a higher average debt interest rate (6.4% vs 5.8% in Q1 2026).
The average debt balance rose modestly in the quarter (+3.5% QoQ, +5.3% YoY).
Overall conclusions:
Q2 2026 results were strong and in line with our expectations, with H1 core NPAT-MI reaching
52% of our full-year forecast. We currently see no material changes to our forecast.
For the Q3 outlook, we had initially expected margins to ease QoQ, as easing Middle East
tensions were expected to drive lower prices for both steel inputs and outputs. Tensions have
since escalated again, however, and we believe the outlook now hinges on how the conflict
develops: if it persists, we expect Q3 margins to remain resilient vs Q2; if it proves short-lived,
we expect Q3 margins to ease QoQ as previously anticipated. In short, the swing factor for Q3
margins, in our view, is the trajectory of the conflict in the Middle East.
