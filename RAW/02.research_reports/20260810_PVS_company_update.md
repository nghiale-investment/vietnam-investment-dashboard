## Stronger 2026 profit; larger long-term backlog amid upcycle

- We lower our target price (TP) for PVS by 2.0% to VND58,800/share, while reiterating a BUY rating. The lower TP is mainly due to (1) a 1.0-ppt increase in our risk-free rate assumption to 7.0%, outweighing (2) a 1.3% increase in our aggregate 2026–2030F NPAT-MI forecast (changes of +18%/-6%/-2%/-3%/+5% for 2026-30F, respectively).

- The higher aggregate reported NPAT-MI is mainly driven by (1) 18.5% higher 2026-2030 mechanical & construction (M&C) backlog assumption, (2) adding one-off compensation income and a larger guarantee provision reversal in 2026, and (3) a 7% increase in aggregate 2026-2030 net FX gains. These positives outweigh higher SG&A expenses, reflecting H1 2026 cost trends and Circular 99/2025/TT-BTC reclassification.

- We forecast 2026F reported NPAT-MI to rise 20% YoY, driven by (1) 35% YoY higher M&C revenue, and strong net other income. For 2027, we forecast core NPAT-MI to grow 23% YoY, driven by (1) 18% YoY higher M&C revenue; (2) M&C GPM expanding 0.5 ppts YoY, supported by improving offshore wind execution efficiency and a Block B peaking progress, and (3) 36% YoY higher financial income on rising net FX gains as international revenue climbs.

- We project reported/core EPS CAGRs of 15%/26% for 2026-28F, driven by M&C backlog projection of USD7.0bn for 2026-30F and profit from FSO/FPSO JVs increasing from VND729bn in 2026F to VND995bn in 2028F. PVS’s valuation is attractive at a reported 2026 P/E of 9.8x (implied PEG of 0.7) vs its 5-year average P/E of 17.8x.

- Upside potential/catalysts: Winning a USD3bn contract in Qatar (page 18), higher-than-expected reversal of warranty provision (VND4.1tn; end-Q2 2026), 20% stock dividend, HOSE listing.

- Downside risk: Lower-than-expected M&C margin. Compensation income and provision reversals to lift 2026 earnings. In Q2 2026, PVS booked VND459bn of compensation income, which we attribute to the cancellation of a 35-jacket/foundation EPC contract under the Formosa offshore wind project in Taiwan (above our VND100bn assumption). In addition, PVS has not yet recognized any guarantee provision reversal in H1 2026 (guided at VND635bn vs our VND150bn forecast), of which around VND600bn relates to the Sao Vang–Dai Nguyet project, where PVS expects to sign the final acceptance this year. We therefore raise our 2026F net other income forecast to VND1.1tn (from VND250bn). We raise our 2026–2030F M&C backlog assumption by 19% to USD7.0bn. PVN’s targeted Blue Whale project to restart in Q4 2026, which supports FID in H2 2027/H1 2028 (earlier than our previous assumption of H2 2028); while PVS expects Golden Sea Lion to reach FID in 2027 (one year ahead of our previous assumption). Besides, we expect domestic E&P to strengthen further with amended Oil & Gas Law (expected to be approved in August 2026), and the decree on special mechanisms for PVN in H2 2026). M&C  backlog assumptions

We raise our 2026-2030F M&C backlog assumption to USD7.0bn (+18.5% from USD5.9bn). The increase mainly reflects (1) a one-year forward shift and a larger scope for Blue Whale (USD641mn recognized within 2026-30F vs USD318mn previously), (2) a larger offshore wind workload during 2026–2030, including USD1,700mn from overseas projects (vs USD1,400mn previously) and USD800mn from domestic projects (vs USD400mn previously), reflecting PVS’s new guidance, strategy (page 18) and a brighter offshore wind power outlook (page 15). We also assume annual onshore project backlog of USD50–100mn, as PVS plans to expand its EPC business.

PVS raised its 2026-2030 revenue guidance to VND210tn-220tn (~39% above the previous guidance of VND150tn-160tn released in October 2025). The new range is 98%-108% above aggregate revenue during 2021-2025 underscoring the company’s robust earnings visibility. For 2030 alone, PVS targets revenue of VND60tn, implying a 2025-2030 revenue CAGR of 13%. During the June 2026 Analyst Meeting (AM), PVS shared that its estimated 2027 backlog is equivalent to around 70% of its expected execution value for 2026, more than VND20tn. This implies secured backlog of around VND28.6tn/VND20tn for 2026/2027, equivalent to 98%/58% of our respective M&C revenue forecast. That said, management noted that PVS is continuing to

actively bid for new projects to expand its workload. PVS’s medium-term M&C outlook remains resilient: We estimate PVS has secured USD2.1bn in signed contracts (30% of our 2026-2030F backlog assumption). Signed contracts include USD1.0bn from offshore wind projects (28% of our offshore wind backlog assumption) and USD1.1bn of oil & gas projects (i.e., USD675mn from Block B, USD102mn from Yellow Camel, and USD364mn from White Lion Phase 2B).

## Long-term forecast changes

We raise our aggregate 2026–2030F NPAT-MI forecast by 1.3%, mainly driven by (1) an 18.5% increase in our 2026–2030F M&C backlog assumption; (2) one-off compensation income and a larger guarantee provision reversal, which mainly benefit 2026 earnings; and (3) a 7% increase in our aggregate net FX gain assumption to VND2.9tn. The higher FX gain reflects a larger contribution from overseas M&C projects, which increases PVS’s USD-denominated revenue while a significant portion of its costs remains VND-based. These positives outweigh our higher SG&A expense assumption, reflecting H1 2026 cost trends and the impact of the Circular 99/2025/TT-BTC reclassification.

## Valuation

In this Update Report, we continue to use the 100%-discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation model as we believe the DCF approach is the most suitable method to reflect the intrinsic value of PVS. Additionally, we maintain our 5% discount for limited transparency to our valuation.

We lower our target price (TP) for PVS by 2.0% to VND58,800/share, while reiterating a BUY rating. The decrease is driven by (1) a 1.0-ppt increase in our risk-free rate assumption to 7.0%, which lifts our WACC to 15.2% from 14.4%, outweighing (2) a 1.3% increase in our aggregate 2026-2030F NPAT-MI forecast.
