## Sustained passenger growth, expecting oil headwinds to ease

- We lower our target price (TP) by 10% and reiterate our MARKET PERFORM rating for ACV. Over the past three months, ACV’s share price has declined 10%.

- Our lower TP mainly reflects a higher WACC assumption of 11.7%, vs 11.1% previously. This is driven by a 100-bps increase in our risk-free rate (RFR) assumption to 7.0%, in line with Vietcap’s internal adjustment (see Valuation). Additionally, we keep our earnings forecasts unchanged, projecting NPAT-MI growth of +1%/-51%/+48% YoY in 2026F/27F/28F.

- We maintain our international passenger (pax) forecasts at 49/54/61mn in 2026F/27F/28F, implying growth of +3%/+12%/+13% YoY. We maintain our domestic pax forecasts at 74mn/82mn/89mn in 2026F/27F/28F, implying growth of +1%/+10%/+9% YoY.

- On a like-for-like basis excluding PQC’s contribution in 2025, our 2026F pax forecasts imply stronger underlying growth, with international and domestic pax rising 10% and 5% YoY, respectively, despite cautious demand assumptions amid Middle East tensions.

- As a reminder, our 2026 forecast onward excludes PQC’s contribution (see Update Report).

- We maintain our projection for Long Thanh Airport (LTH) opening in H1 2027, with an assumed pax service charge of USD25/pax, in line with Noi Bai and above Tan Son Nhat’s USD20/pax.

- Downside risks: Higher-than-expected bad debt provisions/capex; slower-than-expected airport expansion; lower-than-expected pax numbers; dilution from injection of State-invested assets; headline risk related to ACV’s investigation under central oversight.

- Upside potential: One-off compensation for PQC: ACV has completed the asset valuation and submitted a compensation proposal to authorities, pending a fuller review. At YE2025, PQC’s net book value was VND762bn vs a historical cost of VND3.2tn. Higher oil prices pose near-term pressure, but we expect conditions to ease toward Q4 2026. Our Energy team raised their 2026 Brent base-case assumption to USD83/bbl from USD75/bbl and trimmed the high case to USD93/bbl from USD95/bbl (see Sector Report, on July 24, 2026), amid prolonged US–Iran tensions. For 2027, the team forecasts Brent at USD70/bbl in the base case and USD80/bbl in the high case as Hormuz traffic normalizes and the oil market moves into surplus. The base case assumes Hormuz flows recover to 50%/90% of normal levels in August/October, while the high case assumes 20%/50%/90% in August/October/December. Despite the slower recovery assumption, stronger-than-expected Q2 pax traffic offsets the prolonged oil-price headwind, and so we keep our scenario analysis unchanged (see page 4). Vietnam’s aviation traffic remains in-line with our pax forecast. Per CAAV, total pax throughput across Vietnam’s airports was 76mn in 7M 2026 (+7% YoY), comprising 30mn international pax (+11% YoY) and 46mn domestic pax (+4% YoY). This growth suggests that the impact of higher fuel costs and airfares on travel demand has remained manageable so far.

Long Thanh Airport (LTH) Phase 1 inspection conclusion On July 28, 2026, the Government Inspectorate identified multiple procedural, contractual, and construction-management violations at LTH Phase 1, including ACV-managed Sub-project No. 3.

For ACV, the main findings involved Package 5.6 for passenger-terminal piling and Package 5.10 for terminal construction and equipment installation. - Potentially unauthorized subcontracting was the most serious finding. At Package 5.10, seven main contractors transferred around VND1.6tn of work to contractors not approved by ACV as subcontractors. At the Vietur consortium level, nine of ten members were involved in approximately VND1.7tn of work classified as prohibited contract transfers. Similar issues involved VND87bn at Package 5.6.

- A key financial issue relates to USD235.7mn of equipment costs under Package 5.10. The Vietur consortium bid and contracted this portion in USD, which the Inspectorate deemed inconsistent with bidding regulations. ACV was instructed to determine a compliant conversion rate and review all remaining foreign-currency equipment payments to prevent losses to State capital.

- Other findings included weaknesses in tendering and bid evaluation, changes in material/equipment specifications without corresponding price adjustments, incomplete testing and insurance procedures, construction delays, quantity discrepancies, and improper use of advance payments. Eight Vietur members placed VND936bn of advances in term deposits, earning VND10.7bn of interest. - The Inspectorate referred information to the Ministry of Public Security for further investigation and handling. The referral included prohibited contract transfers, bidding and

payment-currency violations, and potential procurement-related violations. However, the public notice does not determine criminal liability for specific individuals or quantify an aggregate financial loss attributable to ACV. - ACV is required to implement corrective measures, including reviewing subcontracting arrangements and contractor qualifications, recalculating contract quantities and prices, recover or adjust payments where necessary, address technical and safety issues, and strengthen construction-progress monitoring. ACV must also closely monitor construction progress and reallocate work if contractors fail to meet their committed schedules.

- Our view: - We view the inspection findings as neutral to slightly negative for ACV. The violations are broad and serious, particularly the scale of prohibited contract transfers and the referral of several matters to the Ministry of Public Security. However, the public notice does not identify any newly charged ACV executives. Key ACV executives associated with the existing criminal case have already been charged and remain under investigation. - No material financial loss, penalty or compensation obligation for ACV has been quantified. Most remedies involve recalculating contract values, reducing or recovering inappropriate payments and preventing future losses, which could partly protect project economics. The USD-denominated equipment costs remain the most notable direct financial risk, although most had not been paid as of the inspection cut-off date, giving ACV room to adjust the payment mechanism and mitigate the potential FX impact. - Near-term risks primarily involve stricter payment controls, additional contractor and documentation reviews, and potential execution delays. However, the conclusion does not order a suspension of construction. Instead, it requires ACV to closely monitor progress, resolve technical issues and reallocate work where contractors fail to meet their commitments. We therefore currently see no sufficient basis from the inspection conclusion alone to revise our earnings forecasts or LTH operation assumptions.

## Valuation

We maintain our valuation methodology based on a blend of DCF (70% weighting) and Enterprise Value/Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization (EV/EBITDA, 30% weighting). For our DCF, we maintain a 13-year explicit forecast period (2026F–2038F) to capture ACV's long-term growth potential from LTH Phase 1 (expected to launch in H1 2027) and Phase 2 (expected construction in 2028–2032). Our DCF valuation is heavily skewed toward the terminal value estimate due to a high projected capex burden over the next decade. In this Update Report, we raise our risk-free rate (RFR) assumption by 100 bps to 7.0%, in line with Vietcap’s internal adjustment. While the State Bank of Vietnam is expected to keep policy rates unchanged in the near term, we anticipate a structurally higher domestic yield environment over the medium term. Accordingly, our WACC increases to 11.7% from 11.1% previously. We maintain a 30% weight of the EV/EBITDA valuation, applying an unchanged EV/EBITDA target multiple of 16.0x. This is slightly lower than ACV’s 2017-2025 average (excluding 2020- 2022) of 17.6x, reflecting Government support for private participation increases, leading to a more diversified competition landscape beyond ACV’s traditional monopoly structure.
