## Softening retail credit, higher rates pressure core earnings

- We lower our target price (TP) for VIB by 12.3% to VND16,100/share and maintain our OUTPERFORM rating. Our lower TP is due to (1) reducing our target P/B multiple from 1.2x to 0.9x, reflecting our projection for deteriorating asset quality in 2027F, and (2) an 18.7% decrease in our aggregate 2026-2030F NPAT-MI (-13.6%/-22.5%/-22.1%/-20.5%/-15.4% in 2026/27/28/29/30F) that more than offset the positive impact from rolling forward our forecast period to mid-2027F.

- We lower our forecast for 2026F NPAT-MI by 13.6% mainly due to (1) H1 2026’s weaker-than-expected core earnings with sluggish credit growth and (2) the bank’s shift to the corporate sector which has a lower yield, driving down 2026F NIM by 11 bps YoY.

- VIB is currently trading at a forward 2026F P/B of 0.93x – a 19.8% discount, compared to its peer median of 1.16x with a 2026F ROE of 17.0% vs a peer median ROE of 17.6%, per our forecast. However, VIB’s 2026 earnings are partly supported by one-off income.

- Downside risks: Slower-than-expected credit growth, further NIM compression. Upside potential: Higher-than-expected recovery income. We forecast VIB’s 2026F credit growth at 12%, mainly driven by the corporate sector. Under the higher interest rate environment in H1 2026, we observed that credit demand for the retail segment relatively weakened compared to the corporate sector. Specifically, for VIB, in H1 2026, retail loans reduced by 2.02%, while corporate exposure increased 17.76% from end-2025’s base and the bank’s total credit grew only 3.96% out of its 12% quota. To fully utilize the remaining 8%, the bank’s management team expects to focus on the corporate sector in H2 2026. We believe this target is achievable given large credit demand from conglomerates. We expect the bank’s NIM to remain suppressed in 2026-2027 due to more constraints in credit growth and remaining high funding costs. In H1 2026, VIB’s real estate exposure already increased by 11.2% compared to end-2025. As the SBV has recently tightened credit controls on the real estate sector to ease interest rate pressure, we believe the bank’s ability to disburse to the sector in H2 remains limited. Therefore, we expect VIB’s NIM to remain compressed in 2026- 2027 as the bank shifts toward lower-yielding, non-real estate corporate loans, while more competitive deposit offerings may limit the decline in funding costs. We expect ROE to remain under pressure through 2027F, before recovering in 2028F. Despite recognizing ~VND2tn of one-off income in H1 2026, VIB completed only 45% of its full-year PBT target. While management retains its high PBT growth target of 27% YoY on stronger H2 recoveries following accelerated write-offs, we remain more cautious given persistent high interest rates, which could constrain recoveries, alongside further NIM compression. We forecast ROE to decline to 13.1% in 2027F from our estimated 2026F core ROE excluding one-offs of 14%, highlighting limited improvement in underlying profitability.

## Valuation

We utilize two valuation methodologies in estimating our target price, assigning equal weighting to the residual income method and targeting P/B multiple. We lower our target price (TP) for VIB by 12.3% to VND16,100/share and maintain our OUTPERFORM rating. Our lower TP is due to (1) reducing our target P/B multiple from 1.2x to 0.9x, reflecting our projection for deteriorating asset quality in 2027F, and (2) an 18.7% decrease in our aggregate 2026-2030F NPAT-MI (-13.6%/-22.5%/-22.1%/-20.5%/-15.4% in 2026/27/28/29/30F) that more than offset the positive impact from rolling forward our forecast period to mid-2027F.

We note that we have also lowered our dividend per share (DPS) projection from VND1,000/share to VND500/share in 2026F and 2027F, as we believe ROE deterioration and funding pressure in the coming years will motivate the bank to preserve more equity to maintain its current LDR level while achieving further growth.

The above sensitivity matrix illustrates a fair value P/B multiple for VIB at 1.17x using the Gordon Growth Model (GGM) with our median ROE in 2020-2030F at 17.0%, cost of equity at 15.0%, and terminal growth assumption of 3.0% (Figure 6). We note that we employ median ROE instead of average ROE for VIB as we believe VIB’s historical high ROEs of around 30% from 2020 – 2022 are not representative of the bank's future sustainable ROE due to (1) increasing competition, especially for tier-2 retail banks and (2) VIB’s shift from the highly profitable retail sector toward lower yield corporate loan changes the nature of the bank’s business. In this Update Report, our target P/B for VIB is 0.9x, which represents a discount on the fair value P/B derived from the GGM to reflect our conservative view on the near-term weaker core business of VIB due to the high-interest rate environment increasing the risk of more NPL formation. We note that the bank’s current foreign ownership limit (FOL) is currently capped at 4.99%. While the bank has not announced an external equity-raising plan, a potential increase in its FOL could provide strategic flexibility to bring in foreign capital or a strategic investor in the longer term.
