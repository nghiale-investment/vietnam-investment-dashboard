 Best-in-class private bank trading at bargain price
 • We maintain our BUY rating and target price of VND42,600/share as the positive impact from
  (1) a 6.2% increase in our aggregate 2026-2030F earnings forecast is offset by (2) a higher
  COE due to a higher risk-free rate (RFR) assumption.
 • Downside risks: Weaker-than-expected NIM, higher-than-expected NPL.
 • Upside catalyst: Higher-than-expected credit growth.

 Slower activities in the real estate market due to higher rates may weigh on asset quality,
 but impact should be manageable. At the beginning of the year, we suspected that as a
 growing portion of mortgages with fixed favorable rates turn into higher floating rates after the
 grace periods end, asset quality pressure may arise. In H1 2026, we have seen a modest uptick in
 TCB’s mortgage NPL ratio (1.97%, +13 bps YTD). Such pressure may continue in H2 2026 and
 2027, but we do not believe it will affect TCB’s ability to deliver strong earnings growth given (1)
 the bank’s focus on affluent retail customers and high-quality developers/projects, and (2) the
 resilience of the overall book with a low end-Q2 2026 NPL ratio of 1.15% (+2 bps YTD) and a robust
 end-Q2 2026 LLR buffer of 126%. TCB’s rigorous underwriting discipline was proven in the
 previous cycle as the bank’s average credit costs pre/post-recoveries were only 0.76%/0.47%
 during 2020-2025, respectively.

 Infrastructure (infra) lending has emerged as a major credit growth driver. In June, 18 major
 infra projects with total borrowing needs of USD28.8bn were granted exemption from credit
 quota by the SBV. In July, another 17 projects with total borrowing needs of USD36.5bn were also
 granted special quota mechanisms. The developers of these projects including Vingroup,
 Masterise, Sun Group, and THACO; are all TCB’s customers with Vingroup and Masterise being
 two key partners. Therefore, we expect TCB to benefit disproportionately from credit
 opportunities associated with these projects. On funding adequacy concerns, we expect banks
 to primarily finance the construction phase. Once the assets are operational and generating
 cash flows, the owners can refinance the bank debts with cheaper offshore funding from
 investors such as development finance institutions (DFIs) like IFC, DFC, ADB, etc. or infra funds.

 This is a common practice globally and Vietnam saw similar deals in the past such as Mong Duong
 II Thermal Power Plant or Dakdrinh Hydropower Plant. With respect to profitability, the NIM of
 infra projects is lower than real estate lending but the return on RWA could be on par, per TCB.
 TCB’s leadership positions in terms of CASA ratio, fee generation capabilities, technology,
 and productivity warrant a premium valuation. These structural competitive advantages will
 help TCB compound its earnings at a higher rate than the sector in the long run. Therefore, we
 find the stock highly attractive at a 2026 P/B of only 1.13x vs its peer median of 1.17x. As TCB
 continues to deliver strong earnings performance relative to peers in upcoming quarters like the
 bank did in H1 2026, we expect the stock to re-rate higher accordingly.

                    Valuation

                    We utilize two valuation methodologies to derive our estimated mid-2027 target price, including
                    the residual income method with 50% weighting and the target P/B method with 50% weighting.
                    We maintain our BUY rating and target price of VND42,600/share as the positive impact from (1) a

                    6.2% increase in our aggregate 2026-2030F earnings forecast (respective increases of
                    0.4%/1.8%/3.2%/9.1%/11.6% for 2026/27/28/29/30F) is offset by (2) a higher COE assumption due
                    to a higher risk-free rate assumed.
                    Our higher 2026-2030F earnings forecast is mainly driven by a 6.3% increase in aggregate NII as
                    we raise our 2026-2028F credit CAGR assumption from 16.0% to 20.0% to reflect stronger-than-
                    expected H1 2026 results and TCB’s participation in multiple infra projects exempt from credit
                    quota. However, we lower our NIM assumptions slightly for the 2027-2030F period to reflect higher
                    assumed COF and dilution impact from higher weight of lower-margin infra loans. The increase in
                    aggregate NII is partially offset by a 21.1% increase in aggregate provision expenses, driven by

                    stronger loan growth assumed and slightly more conservative asset quality assumptions.
                    We continue to apply a specific beta assumption to TCB, which is estimated based on trailing two-
                    year raw betas from Bloomberg with a 50% weighting and a beta of 1.0, also with a 50% weighting,
                    to reflect the assumption that the beta should revert to the mean over the long run.

                    In this Update Report, we increase our RFR assumption by 100 bps to 7.0%. While the SBV’s policy
                    rate remains unchanged in the near term, we forecast a structurally higher domestic yield
                    environment over the medium term. This upward revision is driven by two factors:
                     •  Domestic infrastructure funding: Vietnam's robust credit demand, driven by a strong
                        pipeline of large infrastructure projects, will necessitate substantial long-term capital
                        mobilization, putting structural upward pressure on rates.
                     •  Global macro headwinds: We observe sustained, structural upward pressure on global

                        benchmark rates, limiting any room for domestic yield compression.
