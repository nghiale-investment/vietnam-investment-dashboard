PTB - H1 NPAT-MI rises 17% YoY, upside potential to
forecast - Earnings Flash
Company Research 01 Aug 2026
* PTB reported Q2 2026 revenue of VND2.1tn (+9% YoY; broadly flat QoQ) and NPAT-MI of
VND128bn (+2% YoY; -15% QoQ). This brought H1 2026 revenue to VND4.2tn (+19% YoY; 53% of
our 2026F forecast) and NPAT-MI to VND277bn (+17% YoY; 68% of our 2026F forecast). We see
upside potential to our 2026 earnings forecast, pending a fuller review.
* Stone segment:
- Stone was the main Q2 2026 revenue growth driver, with revenue coming in at VND615bn
(+25% YoY; +35% QoQ). H1 2026 stone revenue was VND1.1tn (+22% YoY; 47% of our 2026F
forecast).
- Stone GPM was 26.3% in Q2 2026 (-3.2 ppts YoY; +0.9 ppt QoQ), bringing H1 2026 stone GPM
to 25.9% (-2.8 ppts YoY), below our 2026F forecast of 27.4%. Amid intensifying price
competition, PTB continues to upgrade its product mix toward higher-margin engineered and
quartz stone to support profitability.
* Wood segment:
- Wood revenue was VND1.1tn in Q2 2026 (+9% YoY; -12% QoQ), bringing H1 2026 wood
revenue to VND2.4tn (+17% YoY; 55% of our 2026F forecast). Wood GPM was 19.0% in Q2 2026
(-0.3 ppt YoY; -2.4 ppts QoQ), though H1 2026 wood GPM remained broadly stable YoY at
20.3%, above our 2026F forecast of 17.5%.
- The wood segment continued to benefit from stable furniture demand, including
contributions from PTB’s pallet business which commenced operations in mid-Q4 2025.
Meanwhile, management assesses the US tariff impact as now manageable.
- Wood pallets remain a medium-term growth driver, with current capacity of 90kt/year and a
planned expansion to 500kt/year over the next two years, implying potential revenue of VND2.5tn. Export sales are expected to commence after FSC certification is obtained.
* Real estate (RE): Q2 2026 revenue was VND55bn (-43% YoY; -49% QoQ), resulting in H1 2026
revenue of VND161bn (+29% YoY; 89% of our 2026F forecast) mainly due to Phu Tai Central
Life handovers. H1 2026 real estate GPM was 30.6%, below our 2026F forecast of 40.0%.
* GPM: Q2 2026 GPM declined to 19.4% (-1.1 ppts YoY; -1.7 ppts QoQ), bringing H1 2026 GPM to
20.3% (-0.7 ppt YoY), slightly above our 2026F forecast of 19.3%. The YoY contraction mainly
reflected weaker stone margins and sequentially lower wood margins. Management previously

indicated that input costs had increased by around 5%-6% amid Middle East tensions,
suggesting that margin sustainability will depend partly on PTB’s ability to pass through higher
costs to customers.
* H1 2026 results were above our expectations, mainly driven by resilient wood profitability and
controlled SG&A expenses, which were partly offset by weaker stone margins and nonoperating losses. We therefore see upside potential to our 2026 earnings forecast, pending a
fuller review.
