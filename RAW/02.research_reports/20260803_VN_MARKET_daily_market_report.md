News  & Flash Notes

Vietnam's manufacturing improves as output, new orders rise while inflationary pressure eases

The S&P Global Vietnam Manufacturing PMI rose to 52.9 in July from 51.8 in June, signaling stronger growth momentum in Vietnam's manufacturing sector. Output growth rose significantly in July to the highest level in five months, marking a continuous rise since May 2025 while new orders increased for the third consecutive month. Notably, total new orders were supported by a strong rise in new export orders, with the index increasing at the fastest pace since July 2024. Meanwhile, other aspects also improved, including firms continued to raise purchasing activity and employment rose for the first time in five months while input costs and output prices both rose at the slowest pace since September 2025.

The strong increase in new export orders could be an encouraging signal for exports in the months ahead, which may support domestic production and help to narrow the current record-high trade deficit in 7M 2026.

Inflation continues to ease, but trade deficit widens further in 7M 2026 On August 3, 2026, the National Statistics Office (NSO) released economic data for July and the first seven months (7M) of 2026. Key results are as follows:

- Industrial production: The Index of Industrial Production (IIP) surged 14.5% YoY in July (July 2025: +7.5% YoY), bringing total IIP in 7M 2026 to increase 11.4% YoY (7M 2025: +8.4% YoY), with the manufacturing sector surging 12.0% YoY (7M 2025: +10.1% YoY).

- Retail sales: Total retail sales increased 14.5% YoY in July (July 2025: +9.9% YoY). For 7M 2026, sales rose 13.1% YoY (7M 2025: +9.5% YoY), in which strong growth recorded in retail goods (+12.7%), accommodation & catering (+15.9%), and tourism (+16.3%).

- Tourism: International arrivals continued to increase at +6.6% YoY to nearly 1.7 million in July, bringing the 7M 2026 total to 13.9 million (+13.8% YoY).

- Trade: July exports continued to surge 25.0% YoY to USD53.1bn, while imports sharply rose 41.4% YoY to USD56.7bn, resulting in a USD3.6bn trade deficit. For 7M 2026, exports and imports reached USD319.5bn (+21.7% YoY) and USD340.1bn (+34.8% YoY), respectively, with the trade deficit at USD20.5bn, a reversal from the USD10.4bn surplus recorded in 7M 2025.

- FDI: In July, FDI registrations surged 32.3% YoY to USD3.4bn, bringing the figure in 7M 2026 to USD38.1bn (+58.0% YoY). Meanwhile, FDI disbursement remained solid, increasing 15.4% YoY to USD2.2bn in July, bringing total FDI disbursement to USD15.2bn (+11.8% YoY) in 7M 2026.

- Fiscal balance: State revenue in 7M 2026 increased 16.0% YoY to USD69.8bn (72.5% of the annual plan), while expenditures rose 7.7% YoY to USD51.9bn, resulting in a budget surplus of USD17.9bn. Notably, public investment disbursement surged 23.7% YoY to USD15.9bn, equivalent to 37.4% of the annual plan (vs the five-year average of 38.2%).

- Inflation: CPI fell 0.12% MoM and rose 4.45% YoY in July. Average CPI for 7M 2026 increased 4.39% YoY. July’s data reinforces our view that Vietnam's domestic growth momentum remains resilient. The key macro risk remains the widening trade deficit, which has been driven mainly by strong imports of electronics, machinery, and petroleum products. Although this could weigh on the trade balance and the USD/VND exchange rate if sustained, we expect the trade deficit to narrow during the August–November peak export season as shipments accelerate. Meanwhile, stronger public investment disbursement should continue to support domestic demand and partially offset external headwinds.

The 2026 fleet expansion plan has been delayed by elevated secondhand vessel prices. PVT’s AGM-approved 2026 investment plan includes four vessels across the crude tanker, product tanker, chemical tanker, and dry-bulk segments. However, no vessels had been acquired as of H1 2026. Management explained that elevated tanker rates have made second-hand vessel costs higher. PVT indicated it will prioritize crude tanker acquisitions in H2 2026, potentially alongside an Ultramax-size bulk carrier. As of end-Q2 2026, PVT’s capex was only VND406bn (equivalent to 22% of our full-year forecast), mainly for two LPG vessels acquired by its subsidiaries. Having said that, PVT has a track record of significant capex disbursement in H2,

and therefore we see insignificant changes to our 2026F capex projection, pending a fuller review. The Strait of Hormuz disruption has had a positive net impact on 2026 operations, although 2027 remains more uncertain. PVT stated that the net impact of the Hormuz disruption on its 2026 operations has been positive and that business conditions should remain strong in H2 2026. However, management views 2027 as a more uncertain year. If shipping activity through the Strait of Hormuz normalizes and transport demand returns to pre-disruption patterns, the operating environment could become more challenging for tanker operators, including PVT. Management cited international research suggesting that tanker rates could decline by

approximately 30% from current levels under a base-case scenario. We view such a decline as a natural normalization. During 7M 2026, time-charter rates increased 74% YoY for Aframax crude oil tankers, 38% YoY for medium-range product tankers, and 32% YoY for Handy chemical tankers, reflecting a strong rally. As a re, even a 30% decline would represent normalization from a high base. We see insignificant changes to our 2027 forecast, pending a fuller review. Middle East exposure remains highly manageable. PVT currently has only one vessel positioned inside the Strait of Hormuz, compared with seven vessels in late February when the disruption began and three vessels in late April 2026. The exposure is therefore limited, with most of the fleet operating outside the region and benefiting from elevated freight rates.
