CTG: Robust earnings growth outlook driven by improving profitability despite
 more moderate credit expansion - Analyst Meeting Note

 Our view: CTG’s H1 2026 results and management’s commentary validate the thesis we flagged
 coming out of Q1 2026, that the bank would prioritize NIM and asset-quality protection over
 aggressive balance-sheet growth this year amid a tougher macro backdrop. Under this strategy,
 we expect stronger profitability to be the main driver of the bank’s robust earnings growth in
 2026. Overall, CTG remains one of our top picks considering the bank’s top-tier execution
 capabilities, market-leading ROE profile, and strong asset quality with a compelling valuation vs
 peers.
 Key takeaways

 1. Macro outlook

 - The rise in imports of means of production is expected to bolster production and export
 capacities in H2 2026. However, significant pressure remains regarding the goal of increasing
 export turnover to balance the trade account, amid rising geopolitical risks, tariffs, and trade
 defense measures imposed by the US, which is Vietnam’s largest export market.
 - Pressure to fully disburse public investment capital is concentrated in H2, particularly for
 strategic and key transportation works.

 - Credit growth for the remainder of the year will be managed flexibly in alignment with macro
 conditions, inflation, and the economy’s capital absorption capacity; capital flows will be
 directed toward production and business activities, priorities sectors, and economic growth
 drivers, while credit extended to high-risk sectors will continue to be strictly controlled.

 - FX pressure in Q3 2026 is likely to remain as intense as in 2025 before potentially easing toward
 the end of the year – a trend largely contingent on the conflict in the Middle East – while demand
 for foreign currency continues to face significant pressure.
 - Deposit rates remain high with no signs of cooling, driven by the economy’s substantial demand
 for capital. Lending rates for priority sectors are being managed at appropriate levels to support
 the economy.

 - Exports are expected to perform better than previously forecast, though significant challenges
 remain: demand from major economies (such as the US) is recovering – particularly in the final
 months of the year – and tariff trends are currently favorable for exporting nations (with Vietnam
 not subject to tariffs related to Russian oil purchases). However, consumer pessimism in the
 Eurozone and the influx of low-priced Chinese goods will significantly impact export
 performance during the remaining months.

 2. Credit
 - 7M 2026 credit growth was 5.6%, a deliberate slowdown vs CTG's historical pace as the bank
 balances loan growth with its funding scale.

 - H1 2026 credit growth by segment:
 + Retail segment: -0.2% (39% of total loans) with household business loans: -0.6% (58.7% of
 total retail loans) and mortgages: -3.8% (28.6% of total retail loans). Retail lending recovered in
 Q2, +2% QoQ.

 + Large corporate segment: +8.8% (31% of total loans).
 + SME segment: +6.5% (23% of total loans).

 + FDI segment: +19.3% (6.5% of total loans).
 - Real estate lending (business + consumer) fell to over 19.2% of total loans as of end-Q2 2026

 from 20% as of end-2025.

 - High-growth corporate sectors in H1: electricity (+7.6%), steel (+7.7%), electrical equipment
 (+25.6%), electronics/computers (+43.7%), and animal feed (+31%).
 - H2 priorities: continue balancing scale growth with funding growth and NIM management;
 direct resources to production/business lending, green credit, and the economy's growth
 drivers, within the SBV's credit quota.

 - Retail strategy: mortgage growth will depend on market demand/absorption;
 production/business lending – CTG's core strength – remains the largest retail growth driver;
 non-real-estate consumer lending is expected to grow strongly, aided by expansionary fiscal
 policy and CTG's digitalized consumer lending products.

 - Public investment/key projects: CTG is prioritizing customers that benefit from public
 investment (construction, materials supply) and proactively engaging project management
 boards under the Ministry of Construction, and provinces to identify contractors and build
 preferential credit policies.
 - On the 18 Government-designated key national projects, CTG has expressed interest, but
 related customers have not yet submitted formal applications to the bank.

 - CTG is part of the lending syndicate for Long Thanh International Airport, though the client has
 so far funded the project with its own equity and has not yet drawn on the bank loan.

 - CTG continues disbursing to contractors on other projects in line with construction progress,
 including expressways (Trung Luong-My Thuan, Khanh Hoa-Buon Ma Thuot, Hoa Binh-Moc
 Chau) and energy projects (O Mon gas-fired power, LNG Quang Trach 2); CTG is also proactively
 targeting ODA-funded projects to grow CASA.
 3. Funding

 - CASA balance was VND444tn at end-Q2 2026 (-3% YTD). CASA ratio fell to 23.5% (-2 ppts YTD),
 with retail contributing ~38% of total CASA. Management attributed the decline to an
 industry-wide trend – corporates terming out deposits to optimize yield, and retail impacted by
 higher time-deposit rates and some household-business cash withdrawals. As of July 30, the
 average CASA balance was VND440tn (+10% YoY), though the period-end balance remained
 slightly below end-2025.

 - CASA breakdown by segment:
 + Large corporates: -8.3% YTD, 16% of the total.

 + SME: -3.5% YTD, 27% of the total.
 + FDI: +10.4% YTD, 19% of the total.

 + Retail: -6.2% YTD, 38% of the total.
 - Q2 2026 regulated LDR: 82.5% (- 100 bps QoQ, -90 bps YoY).

 - Ratio of short-term funds for medium- and-long-term loans: 24.7%.
 - NIM/COF strategy: management welcomed the SBV's move to raise the Treasury deposit
 inclusion ratio in the LDR calculation from 20% to 50%, which has eased LDR compliance; system
 liquidity is expected to improve further into Q3/Q4. If liquidity stays tight, CTG will prioritize safe,
 sustainable growth balanced against funding capacity to protect NIM, rather than chasing

 market share via faster credit growth.
 - Regulatory update – Circular 22 replacement: the SBV has incorporated industry feedback into
 the draft, and the implementation timeline has been extended to 1 October 2028, giving banks
 about two years to prepare. CTG continues to engage with the drafting committee and is
 preparing the data/systems needed to compute and manage the new liquidity ratios.

 4. NIM
 - H1 2026 consolidated NIM was 2.86%, up 29 bps YoY, supported by COF control by increasing
 the share of payment/short-term and FX deposits.

 - H1 2026 net interest income was ~VND39tn (+28% YoY), with growth split roughly 50/50
 between balance-sheet growth and NIM improvement. CTG and the other State-owned banks
 have chosen to compete on cost management rather than deposit rates, which management
 expects to keep gradually supporting NIM.

 5. Asset quality
 - NPL ratio was 1.2% at end-Q2 2026, well controlled and concentrated in sectors negatively
 affected by global and domestic economic challenges such as real estate, consumer lending,
 F&B, and other agricultural products. LLR was 134% at end-Q2 2026, with management targeting
 a build-up to 150%.

 - Recovery income was VND4.4tn in H1 (+11% YoY), completing 44% of the full-year VND10tn plan;
 recoveries are typically weighted toward Q4 as branches concentrate resources late in the year.
 CTG has finalized a recovery model that splits small debts (handled at branch level) from
 complex cases (handled centrally at Head Office).

 - Management is still reassessing the full-year provisioning level depending on how the economy
 and the loan book evolve.
 - Management flagged a prolonged energy-price shock (from the unresolved Middle East
 conflict) as a key risk into H2, which could worsen inflation, FX, interest rates, system liquidity,
 and asset quality. Higher-risk pockets flagged: mortgage borrowers, household businesses,
 micro customers, and unsecured consumer borrowers; and sectors including transportation,
 shipping-cost-exposed exporters (textiles, footwear, wood, seafood), and consumption-linked
 sectors (auto loans, domestic tourism, F&B/hospitality).

 - CTG's response: early identification and review of sensitive segments/sectors, restructuring
 toward customers with strong financials and clear cash flow, prioritizing sectors that benefit
 from public investment (infrastructure, energy), and strengthening resilience via CASA, fee
 income, digital/transaction banking, stress testing, and cash-flow-based provisioning.

 6. Others
 - State ownership: CTG has submitted feasible legal/financial plans to the SBV to raise the
 State's ownership stake from 64.45% toward the Government-directed 65% target, but
 management noted this step alone would not meaningfully increase core capital or affect
 CAR. Separately, CTG has proposed other, more substantive medium/long-term capital-raising
 options to the SBV to sustainably meet Basel capital requirements.

 - Vietinbank Tower: legal procedures have now been completed; CTG and its partner (a leading
 Vietnamese real estate developer) expect to formally sign the transfer agreement in August
 2026, with a public announcement to follow.
