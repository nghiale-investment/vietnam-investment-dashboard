HVN - Q2 swings to loss on fuel-cost shock despite strong
revenue growth; downside to forecast - Earnings Flash
Company Research 01 Aug 2026
* HVN reported Q2 2026 revenue of VND38.4tn (+37% YoY; +4% QoQ) but recorded an NPAT-MI
loss of VND735bn, vs profits of VND2.8tn in Q2 2025 and VND4.3tn in Q1 2026. This brought H1
2026 revenue to VND75.3tn (+28% YoY; 65% of our 2026F forecast) and NPAT-MI to VND3.6tn
(-43% YoY; 42% of our 2026F forecast). We see downside risk to our 2026 earnings forecast,
pending a fuller review.
* VNA’s performance:
- Parent-company revenue reached VND27.2tn in Q2 2026 (+19% YoY; -7% QoQ), including
transportation revenue of VND26.9tn (+20% YoY; -7% QoQ. This brought H1 2026 parent
revenue to VND56.3tn (+19% YoY; 63% of our 2026F forecast).
- International transportation revenue rose 37% YoY in Q2 2026, supported by continued
international network expansion, while domestic transportation revenue declined 6% YoY.
During H1 2026, Vietnam Airlines operated more than 80,000 flights and carried over 13 million
passengers (+4% YoY), including nearly 4.8 million international passengers (+19% YoY).
- Auxiliary revenue remained weak at VND248bn in Q2 2026 (-32% YoY; -2% QoQ), bringing H1
2026 auxiliary revenue to VND500bn (-22% YoY; 38% of our 2026F forecast).
* Subsidiaries’ revenue surged to VND11.2tn in Q2 2026 (+112% YoY; +42% QoQ), mainly driven
by revenue from sales of goods, which increased around 150% YoY to VND9.4tn. This was
mainly driven by SKYPEC, with the growth came from both increased volume and selling prices.
* Q2 2026 GPM declined to 4.4% (-13.2 ppts YoY; -15.4 ppts QoQ). In the quarter, parentcompany total costs rose approximately 42% YoY, materially faster than the 20% increase in
total revenue and other income. In which, fuel expenses increased around 125% YoY, adding
nearly VND6tn of costs amid the Middle East-driven jet-fuel price shock. Managing this impact
was partly offset by a compensation payment from Pratt & Whitney, which was recorded as a
reduction in COGS in Q2 2026, per management.
* Our view: H1 2026 results were below our expectations: Strong traffic, international revenue
growth, and subsidiary contributions were outweighed by the sharp fuel-cost shock. While
HVN has responded by adjusting capacity, prioritizing international markets and managing
costs, earnings visibility remains highly dependent on jet-fuel prices and the pace at which
higher costs can be passed through to fares. We therefore see downside risk to our 2026
earnings forecast, pending a fuller review.
