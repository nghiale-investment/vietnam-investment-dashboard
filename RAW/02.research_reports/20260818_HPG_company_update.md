## Strong earnings outlook intact with attractive valuation

- We reiterate our BUY rating on HPG but cut our target price (TP) by 8% to VND32,700/share. The lower TP is driven by a higher WACC (12.6% vs 12.0%, as we raise our risk-free rate to 7% from 6%), partly offset by -3%/0%/+3%/+5%/+7% revisions to our 2026/27/28/29/30F earnings forecasts.

- The earnings cut is concentrated in 2026F and is mainly driven by a higher SG&A projection, as we raise our selling expenses forecast on higher-than-expected Q2 logistics costs stemming from the Middle East conflict. We expect this pressure to ease in H2 and normalize from 2027.

- Higher 2028-30F earnings reflect the new Tay Ninh EAF construction steel plant (700k tonnes capacity, contributing from 2028F), lifting 2028/29/30F construction steel sales volume forecasts 6%/8%/10%.

- We observe a sharp divergence in steel input prices: on a YTD average basis, hard coking coal prices rose 26.9% YoY against just 5.1% YoY for iron ore, and the gap has since widened (July-1-to-date average: +28.5% YoY vs -2.9% YoY). The main driver is contrasting supply. A fatal gas explosion on May 22 at the Liushenyu mine in Shanxi (China's main coking coal hub), forced shutdowns across 155-160 domestic mines; per Reuters, an industry veteran expects a resulting supply loss of 20-30mn tonnes, equivalent to 13.3%-20.0% of Australia's FY2025-26 coking coal exports. Iron ore, by contrast, faces expanding supply from the Simandou mine, alongside weak Chinese demand. We therefore raise our 2026F coking coal price growth forecast to 20% (from 10%) and cut iron ore to 3% (from 10%), resulting in a broadly neutral net impact on our 2026F margin forecasts. We leave 2027F unchanged, viewing the coal disruption as short-term for now.

- At market price, 2026/27F core P/Es of 8.6x/6.3x look attractive vs a 10Y avg. P/E of 10.5x, 37%/35% core earnings growth, and a 31% 2025-28F core earnings CAGR.

- Upside potential: Stronger-than-expected steel demand; potential earnings contribution from HPG's role in large-scale strategic/national projects, not yet factored in our current forecasts.

- Downside risk: Sharper-than-expected coking coal cost inflation. We expect HPG's 2026F margins to expand YoY, implying YoY expansion in H2, on favorable output-input dynamics. Vietnam's steel prices benefit from capacity cuts under China's anti-involution policy, stricter Chinese steel export controls, and tariff protection against Chinese steel. On the input side, softer iron ore prices should broadly offset higher coking coal costs, keeping the overall raw material basket contained. Near term, Q3 margins hinge on the severity of the Middle East conflict: they should hold up QoQ off a high Q2 base if tensions re-escalate to Q2 intensity, but ease QoQ if the conflict proves short-lived. We see the latter as more likely, as crude oil has not re-tested its Q2 peak despite recent flare-ups, while steel output prices are already trending down, leaving producers exposed to higher-cost inputs procured during the earlier spike. We therefore expect Q3 margins to ease QoQ, while H2 margins still expand YoY on the structural factors above. HPG’s steel capacity update

DQSC2 HRC: Phase 1 came online in March 2025 and Phase 2 came online in September 2025.

DQSC2 – High-quality wire rod expansion (DQSC2-HQWR): On August 7, 2025, HPG received approval to raise its DQSC2 investment by VND3.4tn (USD128.3mn) to VND88.4tn (USD3.34bn) from VND85tn (USD3.21bn). The revised capital structure comprises VND40.8tn (USD1.5bn) in equity and VND47.6tn (USD1.80bn) in debt, vs the original VND40tn (USD1.51bn) of equity and VND45tn (USD1.70bn) of debt.

We estimate VND2.8tn (USD105.7mn) of the additional capital will go to fixed capex and VND600bn (USD22.6mn) to working capital, based on the original capex/working capital split of VND70tn (USD2.64bn) and VND15tn (USD566.0mn) of DQSC2. The approved investment increase will add 500,000 tonnes of annual capacity, raising the total

designed output of DQSC2 to 6.1 million tonnes per year. This includes 5.6 million tonnes of HRC — unchanged from the original plan — and 500,000 tonnes of high-quality wire rod steel, which is expected to come online in Q2 2028. Wire rod is a type of construction steel. As this is high-quality wire rod, we expect its ASP, as well as margins, to be higher vs the traditional construction steel that HPG currently produces.

Railway & specialized structural steel factory: Operation to begin in Q2 2027: Per latest information from local media in early August, the project is now 50% complete after seven months of construction and is targeting Q2 2027 operation, slightly later than the Q1 2027 previously guided. In addition, per management, though the first product is expected in Q2 2027, revenue is expected to be recognized only from 2028, as railway steel requires roughly six months of measurement, testing, and certification before

sales can be booked. We have updated our assumptions to reflect these developments. Given the project's limited earnings contribution, the impact on our consolidated earnings forecast is minor (Figure 5). In the latest development, HPG proposes to expand the project by VND20tn (USD755mn) and raises steel capacity by 2 million tonnes p.a. – but we have not factored this in our forecast as this remains a proposal only.

Project overview and Vietcap’s earnings estimates: Per HPG, the plant will have annual capacity of 700,000 tonnes of railway and specialized structural steel (further capacity breakdown has yet to be disclosed) and planned capital of VND14tn (USD528.3mn). Additionally, the approved investment plan by the local authorities is

VND10tn (USD377.4mn). We therefore assume VND10tn (USD377.4mn) will be allocated to capex, while the remaining VND4tn (USD150.9mn) will be used to fund working capital. As the project’s products are dedicated to national infrastructure, we expect utilization rates to be high but offset by lower margins - a view supported by our preliminary calculations/estimates based on publicly available data. Overall comments on Figure 14: We observe a continued price divergence across both steel inputs and regional steel benchmarks:

- Input price divergence (Hard coking coal vs. Iron ore): Hard coking coal prices maintained strong YoY elevated levels (+28.5% YoY in July 1-to-date average; +26.9% YTD average YoY), contrasting sharply with weaker iron ore prices (-2.9% YoY in July 1-to-date average; +5.1% YTD average YoY). We attribute this divergence mainly to contrasting market fundamentals of the two commodities: coking coal was propelled by big Chinese supply disruptions, whereas iron ore faced expanding supply from Guinea’s Simandou mega-project and suppressed steelmaking demand in China. Regarding coking coal, on May 22, 2026, a fatal gas explosion at the Liushenyu coal mine in Shanxi province (82 casualties) resulted in immediate mandatory shutdowns across 155 to 160 coal mines in China’s primary coking coal hub for emergency safety checks. According to a Mysteel survey reported by Reuters, around 64% of affected production capacity had resumed operations by June 17, but mine utilization rates remained suppressed at 70–80% (well below the 105–110% utilization levels prior to the accident). Per the latest market updates from Shanghai Metals Market (SMM), domestic coking coal output continues to be weighed down by stringent safety supervision and strict approval procedures through early

tonnes due to the accident (per Reuters) — equivalent to 13.3% to 20.0% of Australia's FY2025–26 coking coal exports — which has forced Chinese mills to step up seaborne imports and kept international prices elevated. Conversely, iron ore's higher global supply and weaker Chinese demand resulted in sluggish price performance. Impact to our forecast: As a result, we lower our 2026F iron ore price growth forecast to 3% (from 10%), while raising our 2026F coking coal price growth forecast to 20% (from 10%) - resulting in a broadly neutral net impact on our 2026F earnings forecast. Inputs: We expect input prices to diverge in 2026F, with iron ore weakness (on higher global supply) offsetting coking coal strength (due to domestic supply disruptions in China):

## 1. Iron ore: The primary structural factor is new supply from Africa’s Simandou project.

Simandou, one of the world’s largest untapped high-grade (≈65% Fe) iron ore deposits with over 2.0 billion tonnes of reserves, began initial operations in late 2025. At full capacity (120 million tonnes/year), Simandou would represent ~10% of China’s annual seaborne iron ore imports (≈7.5% of global seaborne trade), signaling a meaningful structural addition to supply. As the mine ramps up and global supply expands alongside weak Chinese steelmaking demand, we expect iron ore prices to remain soft. Because spot prices have performed weaker than our initial expectations, we lower our 2026F iron ore price growth forecast to 3% YoY (from 10% previously).

For now, we view the coking coal supply disruption in China primarily as a short-term issue, and thus do not adjust our 2027F forecast yet, though we continue to monitor the situation.
