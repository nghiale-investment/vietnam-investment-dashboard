## Elevated crack spreads to drive earnings surge

- We raise our target price (TP) for BSR by 8% to VND29,250/share and upgrade our rating from MARKET PERFORM to BUY following the stock’s 20% share price decline since our previous Update Report. We derive our TP from a 50/50 weighted blend of DCF and P/E multiple valuations (previously 100% DCF), adding the P/E method to better capture BSR’s earnings volatility. Higher DCF reflects (1) our 16% higher aggregate 2026–30F reported NPAT-MI forecast and (2) rolling our valuation horizon to mid-2027, which outweigh (3) a higher WACC of 12.5% (vs 11.8% previously) as we raise our risk-free rate assumption by 1 ppt to 7%.

- Our higher aggregate NPAT-MI is mainly driven by (1) raising the 2026 crack spread 11–25% and 2027 crack spread 25%, reflecting supply disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz and a widening global refined product deficit, and (2) a 1.4% increase in 2026–30F sales volume assumptions.

- We forecast 2026 reported NPAT-MI to surge 3.9x YoY, driven by an 86% YoY increase in BSR’s average crack spreads and a 2% YoY increase in sales volume. 2026F reported NPAT-MI is equivalent to 137% of 2022’s level, mainly due to higher spreads, 16% higher volume vs 2022, and 22% higher inventory at the end of 2025 vs end-2021.

- We forecast 2027 reported NPAT-MI of VND12.0tn (-40% YoY) as crack spreads normalize, together with an 11% YoY decline in sales volume following the scheduled sixth turnaround. The refinery product deficit is projected to narrow to 2.4 mb/d in 2027, but this remains above the pre-pandemic average deficit of approximately 2.2 mb/d, supporting tight market.

- BSR’s valuation looks attractive, with a projected 2027F EV/EBITDA of 4.6x (a ~33% discount vs the ten-year average of select regional peers).

- Catalysts: State’s capital reduction (H1 2027) to ensure 10% free float. Downside risks: Adverse oil price movement pressuring crack spreads and/or triggering inventory provisions. The refining market has tightened materially with the global refinery product deficit widening to 3.9 mb/d in 2026 (vs 2.5 mb/d in 2025 and 2.7 mb/d in our previous projection), per the International Energy Agency (IEA). This equals 122% of the 2022 gap and mainly reflects the Strait of Hormuz disruption lasting longer than expected, including refinery shutdowns. Unlike 2022, the current disruption has had a more direct impact on Asia. In 7M 2026, Platts Singapore gasoline/diesel crack spreads jumped 2.1x/3.1x YoY, respectively, averaging 182% of 2022 levels. Middle Eastern downstream infrastructure accounted for the largest share of damage, with repair costs estimated by Rystad Energy at up to USD24bn (~42% of total restoration costs). As repairs could take up to 36 months, downstream capacity may remain constrained even after crude production recovers, keeping refined-product markets tight in 2027-2028. BSR awarded the USD988mn EPC contract for the Dung Quat upgrade and expansion to the China Chengda–Beijing Petrochemical consortium. The project will raise capacity by 15.5% to 171,000 bpd, with completion expected by Q3 2029, in line with our forecast.

## Long-term forecast changes

We raise our aggregate 2026–2030F reported NPAT-MI forecast by 16%. This is mainly driven by (1) raising our assumed efficiency rate to 123% (+3 ppts) following the H1 2026 results, and (2) increasing our average crack spread assumptions by 9%, with respective increases of 18%/25%/0%/0%/0% for 2026/27/28/29/30F. This is further supported by our higher 2026F Brent oil price assumption of USD83/bbl, reflecting a stronger-than-expected refining market amid renewed supply disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz.

Our 2026 gasoline spread assumptions are set at approximately 100% of the 2022 average (vs ~80% previously), while our 2026 diesel and Jet A1 spread assumptions are also set at approximately 100% of the 2022 average (vs ~90% previously). We note that in 7M 2026, average gasoline, diesel, and Jet A1 crack spreads reached 173%/170%/212% of their respective 2022 averages. These assumptions reflect our conservative view that disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz will begin to ease from October 2026 onward.

## Valuation

We derive our target price for BSR using an equally weighted (50/50) blend of a discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation and a P/E multiple valuation. We continue to anchor our valuation on the DCF method to capture the value of BSR’s long-term cash flows, including its upgrade & expansion. We now complement DCF with the P/E multiple method to better factor in the earnings volatility inherent to BSR’s refining business, which is driven by swings in oil prices and crack spreads.

We raise our TP by 8.3% to VND29,250/share and upgrade our rating from MARKET PERFORM to BUY. The higher DCF component reflects (1) a 16% increase in our aggregate 2026–2030F reported NPAT-MI forecast and (2) rolling our valuation horizon forward to mid-2027, which outweigh (3) a higher WACC of 12.5% (from 11.8%) following a 1 ppt increase in our risk-free rate assumption to 7%.
