# Executive Summary

FPT Retail reported strong 1H2026 growth across both FPT Long Chau and FPT Shop. Consolidated revenue increased 33% year on year, while profit before tax rose 117%, materially outpacing sales. After six months, the group had completed approximately 52% of its full-year revenue plan and 67% of its profit-before-tax plan. Management said the result was above the expectations embedded in its original plan and expressed strong confidence in achieving the full-year targets. It expects 2H earnings to benefit from back-to-school demand, major product launches, holiday purchasing, and the normal seasonal recovery in ICT retail, although it cautioned that some recent revenue growth reflected higher selling prices rather than purely higher unit demand.

Long Chau remained the central growth and profit engine. 1H revenue reached approximately VND21.4 trillion, up 33% year on year. Average pharmacy revenue remained around VND1.3 billion per month, above the 2025 average, even as the chain expanded to 2,639 pharmacies by end-June. Management said same-store sales growth was approximately 10-15%, driven mainly by store traffic rather than a single product category or SKU. More than 200 pharmacies had opened against a full-year plan of around 350, indicating a faster-than-planned first-half rollout.

Management sees government enforcement and formalization as a structural advantage for modern pharmacy chains. Tighter requirements for invoices, product origin, pricing transparency, and regulatory compliance are more difficult for independent pharmacies to absorb. Long Chau is also benefiting from scale, broad product availability, competitive pricing, and customer trust. Management said it did not pass all supplier cost increases to consumers; gross margin remained broadly stable, and profit improvement came primarily from operating-cost optimization and leverage on higher traffic.

Long Chau's customer platform is becoming a more important competitive asset. The identified customer base increased to 36 million from more than 30 million at end-2025. Management cited more than 25 million Zalo official-account users and more than 8 million app users. Digital functions include medication reminders, vaccination records, and a family account that pools spending and reward points across household members. Around 70% of customers were described as loyal or repeat customers, supported by the high share of chronic-disease patients who regularly refill prescriptions. Management said the repeat rate was stable to gradually improving, although it should not be expected to rise by five or ten percentage points every year from an already high base.

The product mix remains pharmacy-led. Drugs account for approximately 55% of Long Chau sales, while supplements and medical equipment account for more than 40%. Management expects drugs to remain above 50% in the traditional format, with some regional variation. Gross margin was described as stable around 23-24%; management explicitly rejected the premise that Q2 gross margin had jumped materially. The strategy is to preserve competitive pricing and absorb part of supplier inflation, while expanding operating margin through lower SG&A and more efficient processes.

The vaccination business performed better than plan in Q2. The network reached 236 centers at end-June, and an average center generated around VND1.5-1.6 billion of monthly revenue. Vaccination contributed approximately 10% of Long Chau revenue in both Q1 and Q2. Centers operating for more than one year were EBITDA-positive, and management later said the chain had reached breakeven at the aggregate store level because more than half of the centers were over one year old. At the company level, however, vaccination still recorded a small Q2 loss after head-office costs and losses from newer centers. Demand benefited from a seasonal disease outbreak; management did not identify the disease clearly enough for the record. Management expects Q3 growth to slow as the outbreak effect normalizes, while still remaining ahead of the original vaccination plan.

Management had targeted approximately 50 new vaccination centers for 2026 but opened relatively few in 1H, which supported store-level maturity and improved the Q2 loss. This distinction is important: mature-unit economics appear positive, but consolidated profitability still depends on rollout pace and overhead absorption. Management did not disclose the exact Q2 vaccination loss.

Long Chau is cautiously extending beyond pharmacy and vaccination into diagnostics. It had six sample-collection points in Ho Chi Minh City and nearby provinces, located within vaccination centers to use existing premises, customer traffic, doctors, and nurses. FRT works with third parties including Labhouse and Gene Solutions. Capital requirements were described as minimal, early customer response as positive, and the current phase as a trial intended to build knowledge before a broader healthcare-platform expansion. Management expects more points by year-end and over the following years but provided no revenue or profitability targets.

The pharmacy network may ultimately be larger than previously communicated. Management expects Long Chau to reach roughly 3,000 pharmacies in 2027 and believes 4,000 is feasible over time. The pace will slow from the aggressive expansion of recent years, and management is testing alternative store formats. It emphasized that 3,000 or 4,000 is not a rigid ceiling: expansion will follow customer demand and unit economics. Pharmacity's latest capital injection and renewed expansion were not viewed as a threat to this plan. Management argued that Long Chau's differentiation now extends beyond store count to its customer database, healthcare ecosystem, institutional relationships, and brand trust among patients, doctors, and nurses.

FPT Shop also grew revenue by approximately 33% in 1H despite maintaining the network at around 621 stores. The gain therefore came mainly from same-store sales, product mix, seasonal inventory positioning, financing programs, and better execution after two years of restructuring. Management said Q2 was the seasonal low point and revenue was below Q1. Nevertheless, FPT Shop moved from a Q2 loss in the prior year to a small profit in Q2 2026. It expects significantly better Q3-Q4 performance from back-to-school sales, a new iPhone, Samsung and other product launches, and holiday demand. The explicit full-year objective is for FPT Shop to be profitable.

FPT Shop is not pursuing another broad store-expansion cycle. It is attempting to raise sales per store through a better product and model assortment, selected exclusive products, home appliances and consumer electronics, flexible financing, 0% installment programs, and MVNO bundles. Management targeted approximately 1.1 million MVNO subscribers by year-end. Installment-related sales represented around 30% of ICT revenue. Apple products accounted for approximately 45% of ICT revenue in Q2, leaving FPT Shop exposed to Apple's pricing and product cycle.

Apple had raised prices only on selected products, mainly iPads and MacBooks, during 1H. Management expected the next iPhone generation to be priced higher based on market indications, but its effect on demand would depend on both the new features and final retail price. Higher prices may reduce unit volume while supporting sales value. FPT Shop should receive only a limited inventory windfall because it does not hold three or four months of Apple inventory and end-user pricing is determined competitively by retailers rather than fixed by Apple.

Competitive differentiation in ICT remains modest. Management acknowledged that larger rivals can secure more exclusive models. FPT Shop's approach is to obtain selected Honor exclusives, bundle devices with MVNO/data plans, use its nationwide store network and customer data, and add home appliances and consumer electronics selectively. It does not intend to match Mobile World store for store or compete solely on price.

The balance sheet improved alongside earnings. Net debt to equity declined from approximately 1.0x to 0.6x, while net debt to total assets declined by approximately 5% year on year. The cash-conversion cycle improved from 63 days to 60 days, supported by supplier relationships, while inventory increased modestly because FPT Shop had built stock to secure availability and mitigate rising memory and component costs. Management expects deeper technology and AI use in inventory management to support further efficiency.

Overall, the meeting reinforced a two-engine investment thesis. Long Chau is sustaining high traffic, double-digit same-store growth, rapid but disciplined expansion, and operating leverage while building a broader health-services platform. FPT Shop is no longer the same drag it was during restructuring, but its margins remain thin and seasonal. The most important proof points for 2H2026 are whether Long Chau can maintain traffic-led growth without sacrificing pricing, whether vaccination losses continue to narrow after outbreak-driven Q2 demand, whether FPT Shop converts strong same-store sales into meaningful profit, and whether price inflation begins to weaken ICT unit demand.
