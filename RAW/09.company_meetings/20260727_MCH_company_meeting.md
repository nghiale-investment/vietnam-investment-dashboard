# Executive Summary

Masan Consumer presented 2Q2026 as a volume-led return to sustainable double-digit growth after completing the 2025 distribution transformation. Revenue reached VND7,165 billion, up 14.2% YoY. Management attributed roughly 10 percentage points of growth to volume and about four points to pricing, with the April price increase not yet fully reflected across channels. EBITDA grew 12.8% and NPAT grew about 10.2%, lagging revenue because logistics costs rose, some factory tax incentives rolled off, and certain high-sugar products no longer qualified for the same incentives. Official commentary showed gross margin broadly stable YoY at 44.6% but net margin down 80 bps to 20.7%, mainly because logistics costs increased to 4.4% of revenue.

The channel picture was broad-based. GT revenue grew 10.6% and still represented around 80% of MCH sales. MT grew 28.9%, HoReCa 29.7%, international business 24%, and e-commerce grew rapidly from a small base. Retail Supreme reached approximately 550,000 active selling outlets in 2Q, up from about 430,000 in 1Q as MCH expanded into on-premise outlets. SKUs per order rose from 5.5 to 5.8; approximately 43,000 outlets sold more than six categories; and 30,000 priority outlets contributed about 40% of sales. Management said new products could reach roughly 70,000 outlets in two weeks. The next objective is productivity rather than only coverage, including lifting SKUs per order toward seven.

Seasonings were the leading category, growing 17.1% with a 54.2% gross margin. Management expects both CHIN-SU and Nam Ngư to sustain double-digit growth in 2H2026, with CHIN-SU around 12% and Nam Ngư at least 10%. MCH disclosed that it purchases about 60% of Vietnam's fish-sauce concentrate output in addition to its own production, supporting claims of scale and quality. Around 300,000 stores sell MCH seasonings with direct weekly service, and management expects 2H seasoning revenue and profit growth of roughly 10-15%.

Convenience foods grew 7.4% with a 39.7% gross margin, but Omachi substantially outperformed. Management said Omachi revenue had been growing around 17-18%, targeted approximately 20% revenue and profit growth in 3Q-4Q, and had reached about 18.2% market share after gaining 3-4 percentage points over 12 months. Omachi's medium-term target is 30% share. MCH remained the number-two convenience-food company but had narrowed the gap with the leader to around seven percentage points and aimed to become number one by end-2027. Kokomi remained resilient in the value segment.

Bottled beverages grew 17.8% with a 48.6% gross margin. MCH highlighted Wake-Up 247's strong position in coffee-flavored energy drinks, a new 50ml “One Shot” format, and the relaunch of five Bup Non tea SKUs using external R&D experts. Management wants beverages to become MCH's third major pillar alongside seasonings and convenience foods. Home and personal care (HPC), led by Chanté and Homey, grew 32.5% but gross margin fell to 31.8%, down approximately 10.7 percentage points, because petrochemical-linked inputs and logistics costs rose sharply. Chanté had around 40% share within WinMart during testing, was available in 40,000-50,000 stores, and targeted 100,000 stores next quarter before eventually reaching 200,000-300,000.

Management reiterated a four-pillar portfolio for 2026-2030: seasonings, convenience foods, bottled beverages, and HPC. It intends to keep developing 16 existing power brands rather than create many new brands, using innovation and premiumization to deepen them. CHIN-SU and Omachi are leading premium brands; management also mentioned Nam Ngư and Vinacafé in its future global-brand narrative. A more detailed Go Global strategy was deferred to 3Q2026.

Pricing discipline was a major investor concern. Management said the April increase contributed about four percentage points to 2Q growth but was used only after procurement, manufacturing, logistics, portfolio mix, and other cost actions were exhausted. The increase had not fully flowed through distributors, stores, and modern trade in 2Q; management expected 3Q-4Q to capture the full benefit. HPC margin remained the exception because MCH is not the market leader in every subcategory and therefore raised prices only moderately to preserve share gains. Input prices were beginning to ease, and management expected to provide a clearer path to margin recovery in a later quarter.

Management defended MCH's competitive moat as consumer understanding, product innovation, continued brand investment, and execution at the point of sale. It rejected the idea that high-share brands should stop advertising to harvest profit. CHIN-SU chili sauce was cited at about 70% market share, while both CHIN-SU and Nam Ngư gained share in 1H. MCH said modern-retail expansion benefits the entire supplier ecosystem; it maintains broadly consistent terms and service across retailers rather than favoring WinMart.

On distribution structure, management estimated around 500,000 Vietnamese off-premise GT outlets and nearly one million on-premise outlets such as restaurants, cafés, canteens, schools, hospitals, and hotels. MCH directly covers roughly 450,000 off-premise outlets and is selectively entering on-premise. Wholesaler contribution has fallen from approximately 60% to 30% but is expected to stabilize at 20-25% over the long term because wholesalers remain efficient for small, remote, and fragmented orders.

For 2H2026, management guided conservatively to revenue growth above 10%, EBITDA growth above 9%, and NPAT growth above 9%. Full-year guidance remained revenue growth of 11-15%, EBITDA growth of 9-14%, and NPAT growth of 10-15%. MCH also highlighted its cash-generation and dividend profile: annual payout is around 50% of profit and implied dividend yield was discussed at roughly 4%. Management announced that MCH would enter the VN30 from early August 2026, which should improve passive-fund demand and liquidity.
