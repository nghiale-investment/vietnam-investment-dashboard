# Executive Summary

Vincom Retail reported a strong Q2 and H1 2026 earnings performance and reaffirmed full-year guidance. Portfolio occupancy reached 88.6% at June 30, up 2.3 percentage points year on year and broadly stable quarter on quarter despite normal tenant rotation. Q2 revenue increased 10.8% year on year, leasing revenue rose 9.7%, and like-for-like leasing revenue excluding Vincom Center Ba Trieu increased 11.5%. EBITDA rose 10.4% to approximately VND1.5tn, leasing NOI reached approximately VND1.6tn, and Q2 NPAT rose 30.4% to approximately VND1.6tn. For H1, reported revenue was almost VND4.7tn, adjusted revenue was VND4.95tn after including pro forma revenue from a Q1 non-core investment-property transfer, and NPAT was VND3.2tn, up 33.4%. Adjusted revenue and NPAT represented 49% and 58% of full-year guidance, respectively.

The core operating message was that both occupancy and rent still have room to improve. Management expects portfolio occupancy to reach around 92% by year-end based on leases already signed, with the temporary Q2 decline at Vincom Centers and Vincom Mega Malls attributed to tenants rotating and new tenants requiring three to four months for fit-out. New-lease rental reversion at Vincom Centers and Vincom Mega Malls was around 10% or more in Q2; Vincom Center Dong Khoi achieved more than 15% growth in average rent. Across the whole portfolio, rental reversion improved to approximately 5.6% in Q2 from 2%-3% in Q1, indicating that tenant negotiation pressure had eased.

Management described the long-term earnings model as a combination of improving existing assets and developing a uniquely large pipeline. VRE currently operates about 90 malls with roughly 1.9mn sqm of retail area and has another 20-22 malls representing about 1.6mn-1.7mn sqm in the pipeline. Existing-asset growth should come from higher occupancy, single-digit annual rent growth, renovations, tenant remixing, and subdividing former anchor-tenant space into smaller stores that command higher rent per square meter. Management cited past renovations at Vincom Plaza 3 Thang 2 and Vincom Plaza Imperia that raised occupancy from roughly 50%-60% to above 95%, and said Vincom Plaza Da Nang would be upgraded toward Vincom Center standards.

Vincom Plaza Dan Phuong, located in Vinhomes Wonder City, is scheduled to open in August 2026 with about 25,000 sqm of retail GFA. Committed occupancy, including signed offers and deposits, stood at 93%, above the 90% opening target. F&B and entertainment account for about 45% of its mix, and the expected yield on cost is 16%-17%. Management expects the mall to be the first modern shopping center in the Dan Phuong area and a leading retail destination in its catchment.

VRE's broader secured pipeline comprises about 764,000 sqm for which management said approximately VND15tn had been deposited. A further VND10.3tn of deposits relates to shophouse development. Another roughly 980,000 sqm remains under feasibility study, with estimated development capex of VND15tn-VND20tn; VRE said it will not place deposits on these projects until they have sufficient approvals. Management expects any new deposits to VIC and related parties in 2026 to be below VND2tn, if any, because Vingroup and Vinhomes already have a substantial active development pipeline. Timing beyond 2026 will depend materially on the progress of Vingroup and Vinhomes projects.

The deposit-heavy balance sheet currently supports elevated interest income. VRE said another VND6.2tn was deposited in late 2025 for a shophouse component it expects to acquire at Vinhomes Green Paradise. Interest income is likely to begin declining around 2028 as deposited projects move into operation. Management said current liquidity is sufficient for the existing expansion program, so further asset divestments are not being actively pursued, although VRE remains open to raising capital in different forms for expansion from 2028 onward.

Margins showed two different accounting and operating trends. Q2 gross profit margin improved to 61.1% from 55.5%, aided by a non-recurring depreciation reversal following the acquisition and asset-life reassessment of Vincom Mega Mall Ocean Park. Excluding that reversal, management said gross margin improved by about one percentage point. Leasing NOI margin declined to 68.4% from 69.5%, and H1 leasing NOI margin fell to 69.4% from 71.4%, mainly because energy costs increased by more than 10% and payroll costs rose after a compensation benchmarking exercise in September 2025. Energy pressure reflected cooler mall temperature settings, hotter weather, and an approximately 4.5% EVN tariff increase. Management considers the payroll step-up non-recurring but expects structural energy inflation to persist. Future NOI-margin improvement therefore needs to come from stronger occupancy and rental growth.

VRE continues to shift its malls toward experiential retail. Footfall rose 11.6% year on year, while commercial-street footfall rose 16.3%. F&B and entertainment now account for about 43% of the system, including roughly 50% at smaller Vincom Plaza formats and 30%-35% at Vincom Centers and Vincom Mega Malls. The tenant base remains around 70% domestic and 30% international and has not changed materially, although both groups are expanding. Nearly 140 new brands joined the network in H1 and represented 31% of newly leased area. Management said tenant profitability depends more on category, unit size, floor, mall, and negotiated rent than on whether the brand is domestic or international.

The commercial-street business began contributing revenue in March 2026 but remains small at approximately 2%-3% of total revenue because VRE earns commissions rather than booking gross sales. Management is also exploring new formats, including suburban outlet shopping centers, while retaining flexibility about which models enter the pipeline.

Management said recent board and executive changes do not alter VRE's five-year strategy. The changes are intended to strengthen execution, partnership development, new business products, and the operationally intensive commercial-street initiative. The confirmed record identifies Hoa Tran as CEO, Hien Phat as Deputy CEO, Ha Phat as CFO, Duong Tran as Chief Investment Officer, and Huong Nguyen as Chief Accountant.

## 01:00-04:56 - Q2 Headlines and Vietnam Retail Backdrop

Management said the presentation would cover the macro and retail-property backdrop, operational highlights, and financial performance for Q2 and H1 2026.

Vietnam's GDP grew 8.2% year on year in H1, described as the strongest first-half growth in 15 years. Real retail sales increased 7.3%, and FDI disbursement rose 11.2%, supporting employment, household income, and future retail demand. Inflation eased in June but remained above the government's 4.5% target. PMI stayed above 50 for 12 consecutive months, while industrial production increased 11.2% year on year in Q2.

The composition of spending also became more favorable for experiential retail. Spending on accommodation, F&B, and tourism services had nearly doubled since H1 2022, and its share of total retail sales rose from 10.2% to 14%. Management said this trend supports experiential offerings in its malls.

New retail-property supply increased modestly in Hanoi and remained broadly unchanged in Ho Chi Minh City. Ground-floor asking rents in both CBD and non-CBD locations increased at a single-digit pace, although selected prime VRE assets achieved double-digit rental growth. Vacancy rates were broadly stable. Management also noted Vietnam's move into the upper-middle-income bracket and said the speed of the transition was an important signal for international brands allocating regional capital.

At June 30, portfolio occupancy reached 88.6%, up 2.3 percentage points year on year. Footfall increased 11.6%. H1 adjusted revenue reached VND4.95tn, up 15.8% and equivalent to 49% of full-year revenue guidance. NPAT reached VND3.2tn, up 33.4% and equivalent to 58% of full-year guidance. Adjusted revenue included pro forma revenue from a non-core investment-property transfer in Q1 to make the comparison consistent with the full-year plan. Net debt to equity was approximately 1.1%, leaving substantial investment flexibility.

## 04:56-07:39 - Lifestyle Platform Strategy, Footfall, and Brand Expansion

VRE's strategy is to position Vincom malls and commercial streets as lifestyle and community platforms connecting consumers, brands, and local communities. H1 campaigns included the Vincom Red Sale with 2,246 participating tenants, a Grill & Beer Festival at Ocean City, and a Little Intern summer camp. These programs combined shopping, food, entertainment, and family experiences to support dwell time, tenant engagement, and customer spending.

VRE also used the wider Vingroup ecosystem through the VinClub platform, Green SM, and related applications to combine shopping, transport, and dining promotions and encourage repeat visits. Footfall rose 11.6% year on year across the mall network, while commercial-street footfall increased 16.3%.

Nearly 140 new brands joined the network in H1 and accounted for 31% of newly leased area. Domestic brands led expansion, with 208 brands expanding their existing footprint. International brands were also accelerating their entry into Vietnam compared with the post-COVID period. Management said established retailers allocate a large share of their store networks to Vincom and use its malls for flagship stores and exclusive concepts. Adidas opened a high-tier Brand Center concept, while O Cali Club, an indoor sports/gaming concept, was scheduled for Vincom Mega Mall Thao Dien.

Management expected further flagship openings and first-to-market concepts during the remainder of 2026, reinforcing Vincom's role as a platform for retail-brand expansion in Vietnam.

## 07:39-09:38 - Vincom Plaza Dan Phuong and ESG Update

Vincom Plaza Dan Phuong was scheduled to open in August at Vinhomes Wonder City. The mall has approximately 25,000 sqm of retail GFA and is intended to be the first modern shopping mall in the Dan Phuong area. Committed occupancy, including signed offers and deposits, stood at 93% at quarter-end, above VRE's 90% opening target.

The design prioritizes dwell time, with F&B and entertainment representing roughly 45% of the tenant mix. An open, modern layout is intended to support longer visits. The tenant line-up brings several well-known brands to the district, including GO! supermarket, which is joining the Vincom network for the first time. Management expects the mall's central location in Vinhomes Wonder City to make it the leading retail destination in its catchment.

On ESG, management said sustainability is embedded in four pillars: developing an eco-friendly mall network, promoting responsible consumption, contributing to communities, and strengthening corporate governance. VRE received a Green Leadership award at the 2026 Asia Responsible Enterprise Awards for the third consecutive year. During H1, VRE held 112 events promoting green consumption, converted all labor contracts to digital signatures, and digitized 78% of mall lease contracts.

## 09:38-13:52 - Q2 and H1 Financial Review and Reaffirmed Guidance

Portfolio occupancy was 88.6% at June 30, 2.3 percentage points higher year on year and 0.3 percentage points lower quarter on quarter due to normal tenant rotation. Q2 revenue grew 10.8% year on year and leasing revenue rose 9.7%. Like-for-like leasing revenue, excluding Vincom Center Ba Trieu, increased 11.5%. Growth came from higher occupancy, rental uplift in the existing portfolio, and contributions from malls opened in 2025.

EBITDA rose 10.4% year on year to approximately VND1.5tn. Leasing NOI reached approximately VND1.6tn, up 7.9%. Q2 NPAT reached approximately VND1.6tn, up 30.4%. The core leasing business remained the principal earnings driver, with gross profit up 18.3%. Financial income also increased due mainly to interest on deposits placed toward the end of 2025.

Gross profit margin improved to 61.1% from 55.5%. Leasing NOI margin remained high but declined to 68.4% from 69.5%. Net profit margin increased to 67.8% from 57.6%, reflecting the combined effects of higher gross profit margin and interest income.

H1 reported revenue was almost VND4.7tn, up 9.2%, while NPAT reached VND3.2tn, up 33.4%. Adjusted revenue was VND4.95tn after including pro forma revenue from the Q1 investment-property transfer. This represented 49% of full-year revenue guidance, while NPAT represented 58% of profit guidance. H1 leasing NOI margin was 69.4%, down from 71.4%, mainly due to higher energy and payroll costs. Net profit margin improved to 68.9%.

At June 30, total assets were VND62.8tn. Cash, cash equivalents, and term deposits totaled VND5.8tn, while borrowings and bonds were VND6.4tn. VRE completed its cash-dividend payment on July 22, after the quarter-end reporting date. Shareholders' equity reached VND49.3tn and net debt to equity was approximately 1.1%.

Management reaffirmed full-year guidance. H2 priorities are the August opening of Vincom Plaza Dan Phuong, continued occupancy and rent growth across the existing portfolio, and disciplined execution of the development pipeline.

## 21:50-26:00 - Gross Margin Versus NOI Margin and Cost Pressures

An investor asked why retail-leasing gross margin improved year on year while leasing NOI margin declined and requested the outlook for both measures.

Management said NOI margin better reflects the underlying operating cost trend. Energy expense increased for three reasons. First, VRE lowered the average temperature in its mall system by about two degrees, increasing electricity consumption. Second, management estimated that Vietnam's Q2 average temperature was 1.0-1.5 degrees Celsius higher than a year earlier. Third, EVN increased electricity prices by approximately 4.5% from around H2 2025. Together these factors raised energy costs by more than 10% and reduced NOI margin.

Payroll also increased year on year because VRE benchmarked compensation against the market around September 2025 and raised salaries in some departments. Management views this payroll step-up as non-recurring, whereas energy inflation is structurally more persistent because of rising temperatures and annual electricity-price escalation.

Gross margin was also affected by depreciation accounting. After VRE acquired Vincom Mega Mall Ocean Park, it reassessed depreciation for assets that had previously been depreciated on a straight-line basis while the mall was awaiting transfer. Certain equipment has a useful life shorter than the building's roughly 40-year life, so acquisition accounting and reassessment produced a depreciation reversal in Q2. Management said this benefit is non-recurring. Excluding it, gross profit margin still improved by approximately one percentage point in Q2 2026.

## 26:14-31:26 - Rental Reversion and Domestic Versus International Tenants

An investor asked about rental increases on new leases at Vincom Centers and Vincom Mega Malls. Management said rental reversion was positive at around 10% or more in Q2, depending on category and asset. One driver is subdividing large spaces previously occupied by anchor tenants into smaller units, which raises average rent per square meter. Vincom Center Dong Khoi achieved an increase of more than 15% in average rent during Q2.

Management said Vincom Plaza Dan Phuong is expected to generate a yield on cost of approximately 16%-17%.

Asked whether domestic or international tenants are more profitable, management said nationality is not the main determinant. Rent depends on the specific mall, floor, category, and unit size. Fashion, F&B, and entertainment have different economics. Some domestic brands will pay rents similar to international tenants for prime locations such as Vincom Center Dong Khoi.

The domestic/international mix has remained broadly stable in recent years at roughly 70% domestic and 30% international. Domestic brands have an advantage in expansion because they already operate in Vietnam, and many F&B brands are domestic. International brands are accelerating their entry, but they represented less than one-third of the tenant base even before COVID. Management therefore sees growth from both groups without a material change in the overall mix.

## 31:32-36:51 - Three-to-Five-Year Earnings Drivers and Competitive Position

An investor asked which drivers could improve earnings, profitability, and growth over the next three to five years and how VRE's competitive position might evolve.

Management contrasted the business with the IPO period, when VRE operated approximately 40 malls and a large share of valuation came from development potential. VRE now operates about 90 malls and has another 20-22 malls in the pipeline. Growth should increasingly come from operating assets: improving the existing portfolio and opening new malls.

Management said VRE has the largest development land bank among competitors. The operating portfolio totals about 1.9mn sqm, with another 1.6mn-1.7mn sqm in the future pipeline. Existing-asset growth will come from higher occupancy and rent.

Current occupancy is around 88%, with a target of around 92% by year-end. VRE has raised occupancy at renovated underperforming assets such as Vincom Plaza 3 Thang 2 and Vincom Plaza Imperia from roughly 50%-60% to above 95%. In highly occupied malls, VRE is redesigning layouts to accommodate more small tenants. Historically, anchor tenants represented 60%-70% of net leasable area. Subdividing some anchor space broadens customer experience and raises average rent.

Existing malls continue to produce single-digit annual rent growth. VRE plans regular renovation and tenant-mix upgrades. In 2026, Vincom Plaza Da Nang is being upgraded toward Vincom Center standards so it can attract more flagship tenants. Higher tenant revenue under revenue-sharing arrangements should allow VRE to earn higher absolute rent. Management sees occupancy, average rent, renovations, tenant remixing, and the market's largest development pipeline as the principal long-term growth drivers.

## 37:44-41:33 - Secured Pipeline, Feasibility Projects, and Related-Party Deposits

Future timing depends heavily on the development schedules of Vingroup and Vinhomes projects.

## 43:19-45:01 - Commercial-Street Contribution and Interest-Income Timing

The commercial-street segment began contributing in March 2026. Because the business model earns commissions rather than recording gross merchandise value as revenue, the segment accounts for only about 2%-3% of total revenue.

Management expects most of the deposited development pipeline to begin operating from 2028. As capital is converted from interest-bearing deposits into operating assets, higher interest income should begin to decline in 2028.

## 48:09-49:47 - New Formats, Divestment, and Funding Options

An investor asked whether VRE is interested in suburban outlet shopping centers in Hanoi. Management said it is open to new products and is exploring several business models beyond traditional shopping malls for possible inclusion in the pipeline.

Asked whether VRE would divest additional assets, as it did in 2025, to fund new-project capex, management said current liquidity is sufficient for the active expansion plan and the company is not pushing further divestments. However, VRE remains open to raising capital in different forms to finance expansion as it prepares for 2028 and later years.

## 50:31-52:14 - NOI-Margin Upside and Improving Rental Negotiations

An investor asked whether NOI margin could improve from the current level of around 70%.

Management reiterated that the Q2 decline was driven mainly by higher energy costs. Under a conservative assumption that costs continue increasing at up to a single-digit rate, margin improvement would need to come from revenue growing faster than costs.

Management said this is possible because tenant negotiation pressure eased in Q2. Portfolio rental reversion improved to approximately 5.6%, compared with only 2%-3% in Q1. Together with higher occupancy, stronger rents could improve NOI margin over time.
