# Executive Summary

Nam Long's 1H2026 earnings were modest relative to its full-year plan, but management argued that the timing was expected and that a much larger share of handovers and profit is scheduled for 2H. Parent-company profit after tax and minority interest was described as more than VND120 billion. Revenue recognition included continuing handovers at Nam Long II Central Lake in Cần Thơ and Waterpoint Phase 1, contribution from joint-venture projects such as Mizuki Park and Izumi City, and approximately VND490 billion from transferring the IS1 education land parcel at Waterpoint to Khai Sang Group. Financial expense increased only slightly despite rising interest rates. Cash declined because of project-development payments and cash dividends, while short- and long-term debt decreased; management said the group remained in a comfortable cash position without near-term refinancing pressure.

1H presales reached approximately VND5 trillion. Management presented this as resilient because it was roughly equal to 9M2025 and full-year 2024 presales despite higher mortgage rates and market uncertainty. The figure represented only around 21-22% of the VND23.46 trillion full-year target, so the required 2H acceleration is substantial. Mizuki Park/Trellia Cove and Sol Garden together contributed almost half of 1H presales. The company shifted its sales mix toward high-rise apartments and smaller-ticket products as interest rates rose, while keeping landed products available for a possible recovery if financing conditions ease.

Management did not revise the 2026 presales target and repeatedly said the company would continue trying to achieve it. It nevertheless declined to provide a more feasible numerical 2H forecast and sounded more cautious than at HSC's Emerging Vietnam event in early June. Deposit rates had risen since late April, feeding into higher mortgage rates. Management believes rates may cool in late Q3 or Q4 because the government's 10% GDP-growth objective would be difficult to reconcile with persistently tight financial conditions, but it has also planned for rates to remain high through year-end.

The operating response combines product mix, payment terms, construction timing, and non-retail transactions. Nam Long is prioritizing high-rise and small-ticket products for first-home and real-demand customers. It can extend customer payment schedules and align construction progress with those schedules to protect cash flow and margins, a method used successfully at Park Village during the 2023 downturn. If rates fall, it can shift emphasis back toward landed products. Management also disclosed early-stage discussions to sell entire project blocks to partners; such deals would count toward presales and could help close the gap to the annual target.

Trellia Cove at Mizuki Park was progressing operationally. The high-rise had reached approximately level 15, and two low-rise show houses were completed. Blocks T1 and T2 were around 70% sold, while T3—launched in May and described as the most attractive block—was approximately 35% sold. Across the Trellia Cove high-rise, management cited sell-through around 35-40%. The project remained a major contributor to 1H sales.

Sol Garden, Nam Long's 21-hectare Hải Phòng project inside VSIP, achieved strong presales after obtaining its sales license early in 2026. Many buyers work in the industrial park. Management cited selling prices around VND70-80 million per square meter, implying approximately VND7-8 billion for a 100-square-meter townhouse or shophouse. Customers can pay over roughly two years and may receive interest subsidies. Early land acquisition allows Nam Long to price competitively while targeting a gross margin of up to approximately 30%.

Social housing at Nam Long II Central Lake was another demand bright spot. Phase 3 of EHomeS Cần Thơ launched in June through a purchase-right lottery because demand exceeded available supply, and almost all units were taken up. Handovers at Nam Long II Central Lake should continue through 2H.

Management said 1H handovers were not delayed relative to the internal plan: the company completed more than 90% of its scheduled first-half deliveries. Reported revenue looked back-end-loaded because the delivery plan itself was concentrated in 2H and reflects units sold two or three years earlier. Key 2H revenue and profit contributors are Nam Long II Central Lake, Park Village at Waterpoint, Izumi Canaria at Izumi City, and Sol Garden. Izumi Canaria is targeted for year-end handover, and Sol Garden for December 2026. Management expressed 100% confidence in achieving the full-year revenue and profit targets, which official AGM materials set at VND7.63 trillion of consolidated revenue and VND720 billion of NPAT attributable to parent shareholders.

The presales target is much less certain than the earnings plan. Management emphasized that the target was set by the company and project-management team, not by former CEO Lucas Loh alone, so his departure does not invalidate it. However, the response depended on an exceptional 2H sales ramp, potential block sales, and possibly better mortgage conditions. The transcript therefore supports high confidence in scheduled handovers and accounting earnings, but only qualified confidence in achieving VND23.46 trillion of presales.

The July leadership transition was presented as orderly. Lucas Loh stepped down as Group CEO for personal reasons but remained on the board. Nguyễn Thanh Hương, with Nam Long since 2018 and previously responsible for investment strategy and Japanese partnerships, became Acting Group CEO while retaining her Group CIO role. Founder and Chairman Nguyễn Xuân Quang would work alongside her and return as chairman of Nam Long Land, the project-development company. Đỗ Chí Hiếu would chair Nam Long Commercial Property. Management said the operating strategy, 2026 KPIs, and board structure would not change. The board had recently been approved for the 2026-2031 term.

Land-bank expansion remains active but financially disciplined. Nam Long acquired an additional approximately 13% stake in Elyse Island in July, bringing the stake repurchased from TBS Land across two tranches to around 21%. Management described the transaction as a safer way to expand effective land ownership because the legal status and development pathway of an existing project are better understood than those of a new external acquisition. The two tranches had different prices but formed one negotiated package dating from 2025; detailed consideration was disclosed separately by the company.

Nam Long ADC was close to acquiring a 1.3-hectare EHome site in Hạ Long, Quảng Ninh. It had paid a deposit, described the project's legal status as clear, and targeted closing in Q3. Launch could occur at end-2026, although management's conservative expectation was early 2027. ADC was also in the final stage of negotiating another approximately three-hectare site in northern Vietnam, with a possible Q4 closing. Across the group, management said it was not rushing to spend cash while rates were high and market conditions uncertain; payment schedules are a central negotiation point with land sellers.

Gross margin was temporarily elevated. The real-estate-development gross margin reached approximately 77% in Q2 versus 46% a year earlier. Management attributed the increase to final cost reconciliation for Akari City Phase 2, where the completed phase proved more profitable than prior estimates, and to a high-margin handover mix. The effect is accounting- and mix-specific rather than a new sustainable margin level. Management agreed that the finalization benefit would not repeat in later quarters.

Construction-cost inflation should have limited effect on projects currently under development. Nam Long typically signs fixed-price construction contracts when permits are obtained, locking costs with contractors for around three years and transferring much of the inflation risk. Most active phases were contracted in 2024 or early 2025, so management expects margins to remain protected for another one to two years. Higher materials, labor, and energy costs will affect new phases, and Nam Long may pass part of the increase into selling prices. The P&L effect may become more visible around 2029-2030 when phases launched in 2027 are handed over.

Other project disclosures included no material new milestone at Akari City Phase 3, though an update may be possible in Q3 or Q4. Management estimated additional land-use fees for Waterpoint Phase 2 at approximately VND400-500 billion, still subject to refinement. It also continued monitoring potential policy changes on apartment ownership duration and government support for rental housing, but considered the policy direction an attempt to answer the long-term redevelopment problem for aging high-rise buildings rather than an immediate quantified headwind.

Nam Long continued supporting buyer affordability. Its maximum interest subsidy was approximately 9%. Management said some lenders, including Hong Leong Bank, offered Nam Long buyers mortgage rates around 10%, leaving the customer to pay an effective gap near 1% during the supported period. This tool, along with longer payment terms, should mitigate but not eliminate the effect of high market rates.

Overall, the call highlighted a clear divergence between near-term earnings visibility and sales risk. Scheduled 2H handovers give management substantial confidence in revenue and profit guidance, while the presales target requires a very large acceleration from the 1H base. The investment case rests on execution at Park Village, Nam Long II Central Lake, Izumi Canaria, and Sol Garden; successful conversion of Trellia Cove demand; disciplined use of block sales and subsidies; and continuity under Nguyễn Thanh Hương's leadership.
