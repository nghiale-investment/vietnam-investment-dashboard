# Executive Summary

PVTrans reported a very strong first half of 2026, with 1H revenue of approximately VND10 trillion, up 37% year on year and equivalent to about 61% of its full-year plan. Profit before tax reached VND1,334 billion, up 71% and already around 89% of the annual target, while profit after tax was VND1,066 billion, also around 89% of plan. The acceleration was concentrated in the second quarter: revenue reached VND4,681 billion, up 33% quarter on quarter and 28% year on year, while profit after tax reached VND370 billion, up 78% quarter on quarter and 88% year on year. Management attributed the earnings strength to favorable tanker charter rates, high fleet utilization, fleet expansion, and an unusually supportive April-May rate environment linked partly to disruption around the Strait of Hormuz.

Transportation remained the economic core of the group. Q2 transportation revenue was approximately VND3,192 billion, up 11% quarter on quarter and 13% year on year, while transportation gross profit increased about 60% quarter on quarter and 79% year on year to VND879 billion. Transportation therefore produced roughly 92% of group gross profit. Total gross profit reached VND957 billion, up 56% quarter on quarter and 61% year on year. Trading revenue rose sharply to VND2,059 billion, up 78% quarter on quarter and 36% year on year, and accounted for roughly 65% of the sequential increase in group revenue; however, trading remained much less important to profit than shipping. Floating/storage activities generated approximately VND143 billion of revenue and VND49 billion of gross profit, while other services generated VND265 billion and VND23 billion, respectively.

Management described PVTrans as Vietnam's leading liquid-cargo shipping company, with 67 vessels and nearly or more than 2 million DWT of capacity as of June 2026. About 90% of the fleet operates on international routes, while the domestic business retains strategically important positions: management cited 100% market share in domestic crude-oil and LPG transport and more than 20% in petroleum-product transport. The company transported approximately 3.1 million tonnes of crude oil to BSR in 1H2026. The recording did not preserve reliable additional domestic petroleum-product and LPG volume figures. PVTrans said its fleet had expanded from 41 vessels in 2022 to 67, with aggregate capacity roughly doubling.

The Q2 tanker environment was very favorable but had already begun to normalize by the meeting date. Presentation figures included Aframax rates of roughly USD53,000 per day, MR product-tanker rates of roughly USD27,000 per day, VLGC rates of around USD57,800 per day, smaller LPG carrier rates of approximately USD9,200-11,700 per day, chemical tanker rates of roughly USD16,000-18,000 per day, and improving dry-bulk rates. In the Q&A, management cited current oil/chemical rates around USD18,000 per day, MR product-tanker rates around USD25,000, Handysize rates around USD10,000-11,000, and Supramax/Ultramax rates around USD14,000-15,000. The recording did not preserve a reliable current one-year crude-tanker time-charter figure.

PVTrans benefited from keeping meaningful market exposure rather than fixing the entire fleet. Across the fleet, management said approximately 60% of vessels were employed on time charter, 20-25% in pools, and the balance in the spot market. About half of the product-tanker fleet was in pools, which captured the high 1H market. Most time-charter contracts had been renewed in June, with only a few renewals remaining through year-end. Domestic charter rates were stable, and renewed oil/chemical time-charter rates increased by roughly USD1,000-2,000 per day. For crude transportation, management cited a domestic rate of approximately USD46,000 per day versus an international Aframax rate near USD40,000 after the international market eased. Domestic crude employment had been less attractive during the April-May international spike, but became relatively more favorable as global rates normalized.

The Strait of Hormuz disruption had a manageable direct operational effect. One MR product tanker remained in the region through a pool and continued earning, while PVTrans's other relevant vessels had exited. Management did not report a significant safety or operational interruption. The episode nevertheless helped lift international spot and pool earnings in April and May. By late July, rates had fallen from their peak, so management did not expect crude-tanker earnings to increase materially in Q3 even with all four crude vessels operating. Crude shipping should remain the largest profit contributor, but Q3 would compare against an unusually strong 1H market.

Management expects to continue expanding the fleet in 2H2026, mainly through secondhand vessels around 10-15 years old. The intended classes appeared to include an Aframax or other crude tanker, an Ultramax dry-bulk vessel, and an MR/product tanker, although the exact acquisition list was distorted in the recording. PVTrans hopes to receive two to five vessels, but management repeatedly stressed that execution is uncertain. Secondhand tanker prices are elevated, with some candidates costing more than USD60 million and prices approximately 30% higher year on year, compared with an original crude-tanker budget of around USD50 million. Rising bank interest rates add another constraint. Management said it would not acquire vessels simply to meet a numerical target.

The most strategically significant potential fleet opportunity is crude transport for Nghi Son Refinery. Negotiations and PetroVietnam approval were ongoing for PVTrans to transport crude from Kuwait to the refinery. Although a VLCC would naturally fit the long-haul route, current VLCC prices are high; management indicated that PVTrans might begin with an Aframax solution rather than immediately acquire a VLCC. No final contract, start date, volume, vessel requirement, or return target was disclosed.

Fleet rotation and maintenance will affect near-term vessel availability. PVT Apollo was moved from international service to domestic service to replace PVT Hera during dry docking. Hera was expected to return by late August, after which Apollo should return to international employment. A normal technical dry dock may take around 30 days, but total commercial downtime can extend to one or two months because of scheduling, positioning, and associated maintenance. Management also said PVTrans could dispose of one or two older LPG vessels, but this was more likely over the next one to two years than immediately. The LPG fleet consisted of 21 vessels, including two VLGCs.

Two recently added LPG vessels were discussed in relation to the rise in depreciation. Morning Diana was described as operating under a two-year bareboat-charter arrangement with a total contract value of around USD13 million and a purchase option at the end. Avenir Gas was acquired/owned, with total investment around USD50 million and depreciation over ten years. Management did not accept that the two vessels alone explained the increase in group depreciation, which an analyst described as rising from roughly VND550 billion to VND650 billion, but agreed that fleet additions and dry-dock-related accounting contributed to the cost base.

Segment trends were uneven beneath the strong consolidated result. Crude-oil and petroleum-product tankers were the largest revenue and gross-profit contributors. Management cited figures of approximately 30% for crude and 28-29% for product oil, but the transcript does not establish whether these numbers referred to segment gross margins or profit-contribution shares. Chemical and LPG shipping were stable. Dry bulk was weaker and management did not expect the segment to be profitable in 2H or for full-year 2026. Spot exposure, high costs, and a three-to-four-month lag between market-rate improvement and PVTrans's realized rates were cited as the principal reasons.

Transportation gross margin reached approximately 28% in Q2, the highest level in six quarters versus a recent average near 20%. Management would try to protect profitability but was not confident that 28% could be sustained. Maintenance and dry-dock expenditure, fuel prices, depreciation, and changes in market freight rates are difficult to control. Management nevertheless expected the profitability of major shipping segments, especially domestic crude transport, to remain broadly stable rather than collapse.

Management was confident that PVTrans would meet its 2026 plans and likely exceed the profit target. It was less categorical about the exact revenue outcome because trading activity and the normalization of freight rates can move consolidated sales materially. The disclosed 1H result already covered around 89% of the full-year PBT and PAT plans, creating a large buffer. The key tension for investors is that Q2 margins and market rates were exceptional: the full-year profit plan appears conservative, but extrapolating Q2 profitability would be aggressive.

# Clean Meeting Record

## 00:00-03:30 — Opening, participants, and company overview

Nga Tran of HSC Research welcomed investors to the call and introduced the PVTrans representatives. The lead company speaker was Mr. Trí. He said he was joined by Ms. Lê Thị Nga, Deputy Head of the Planning and Investment Department, as well as Thu and another colleague whose name was not captured by the recording.

Management introduced PVTrans as Vietnam's leading liquid-cargo transportation company. Its fleet serves crude oil, petroleum products, chemicals, LPG, and dry bulk. In addition to marine transportation, the group provides offshore and technical services, including operation and maintenance for FSO/FPSO units, central processing platforms, and wellhead platforms. Other activities include LPG and petroleum-product trading, maritime and logistics services, vessel agency and supply, wastewater services, and technical and safety services.

PVTrans is listed on HOSE. PetroVietnam owns 51% of the company. Management cited more than 34,000 investors, foreign ownership of approximately 12.8%, charter capital of VND5.169 trillion, and a market capitalization around VND9 trillion.

## 03:30-08:50 — Domestic franchise, international fleet, and shipping-market update

Management said PVTrans held 100% of Vietnam's domestic crude-oil and LPG transportation markets and more than 20% of the domestic petroleum-product transportation market. In 1H2026, it transported approximately 3.1 million tonnes of crude oil to BSR. Further petroleum-product and LPG volume figures were mentioned, but their categories and units were not sufficiently clear to preserve as firm data.

The fleet had grown from 41 vessels in 2022 to 67 vessels as of June 2026, with total capacity roughly doubling to nearly or more than 2 million DWT. Management said 55 of the 67 vessels worked internationally and characterized approximately 90% of the fleet as international-route capacity. The domestic fleet continued to serve BSR and other core customers in crude, products, and LPG.

Shipping markets had been favorable, particularly for tankers. Presentation benchmarks included Aframax rates around USD53,000 per day, MR product-tanker rates around USD27,000, VLGC rates around USD57,800, smaller LPG carrier rates around USD9,200-11,700, and chemical tanker rates around USD16,000-18,000. Dry-bulk markets were improving.

The medium-term demand-supply picture differed by vessel class. Management's market slides indicated that 2026 trade or ton-mile growth was negative in several tanker categories while fleet supply continued to expand. Dry-bulk trade growth was around 1.8% against fleet growth around 3.8%. For 2027, LPG ton-mile or trade growth was shown around 16.5% versus fleet growth around 11%, while product-tanker demand and supply growth were both around 6%. Management emphasized that geopolitics and route disruptions can override the simple trade-versus-fleet balance in the short term by changing sailing distance and ton-mile demand.

## 08:50-15:10 — 1H and Q2 financial performance

PVTrans reported 1H2026 revenue of approximately VND10 trillion, up 37% year on year and equivalent to 61% of the full-year target. Profit before tax was approximately VND1,334 billion, up 71% and equal to about 89% of plan. Profit after tax was approximately VND1,066 billion, also around 89% of the annual target.

For Q2, revenue reached approximately VND4,681 billion, up 33% quarter on quarter and 28% year on year. Profit after tax reached VND370 billion, up 78% quarter on quarter and 88% year on year.

Transportation revenue was approximately VND3,192 billion, up 11% quarter on quarter and 13% year on year. Transportation gross profit reached VND879 billion, increasing 60% quarter on quarter and 79% year on year. Trading revenue increased to VND2,059 billion, up 78% quarter on quarter and 36% year on year; management said trading generated roughly 65% of the sequential increase in consolidated revenue.

Floating/storage revenue was approximately VND143 billion with VND49 billion of gross profit. Other services generated approximately VND265 billion of revenue and VND23 billion of gross profit. Total gross profit reached VND957 billion, up 56% quarter on quarter and 61% year on year. Transportation contributed approximately 92% of total gross profit.

Management attributed the result to favorable tanker charter rates, high vessel utilization, and the larger fleet. The strong April-May shipping market, supported partly by disruption around the Strait of Hormuz, was an important component of the quarter's improvement.

## 15:10-19:25 — Q&A: Strait of Hormuz exposure and prevailing charter rates

Other relevant PVTrans vessels had already left the area. No material operational interruption or vessel-safety incident was reported.

Asked about current market rates, management cited oil/chemical tanker rates around USD18,000 per day, MR product-tanker rates around USD25,000, Handysize dry-bulk rates around USD10,000-11,000, and Supramax/Ultramax rates around USD14,000-15,000. A current one-year crude-tanker time-charter figure was also discussed, but the recording did not preserve the vessel class and exact rate reliably enough to retain.

## 19:25-24:55 — Q&A: 2H fleet investment, asset prices, and LPG fleet rotation

Management said PVTrans intended to continue buying secondhand vessels, generally around 10-15 years old. The targeted classes appeared to include crude/Aframax, Ultramax dry bulk, and MR/product tanker capacity. The company hoped to receive between two and five vessels during 2H2026, but the number depended on finding assets at acceptable prices and obtaining suitable financing.

Secondhand tanker prices had risen sharply. Some vessels were offered above USD60 million, about 30% more than a year earlier, while PVTrans's original budget for a crude tanker was around USD50 million. Bank interest rates were also rising. Management stressed that the company would be cautious and would not overpay merely to complete its fleet-expansion plan.

PVTrans had 21 LPG vessels, including two VLGCs. It was considering the eventual disposal of one or two older LPG vessels, but management described this as more likely within the next one to two years rather than an immediate 2H action.

## 24:55-30:40 — Q&A: segment contribution and Nghi Son crude-transport opportunity

Crude-oil and petroleum-product tankers were the principal revenue and gross-profit contributors. Management described chemical and LPG earnings as stable and dry-bulk earnings as slightly lower. It cited approximately 30% for crude oil and 28-29% for product oil, but the recording did not make clear whether these figures were segment margins or shares of total profit contribution.

Management discussed the possibility of transporting crude from Kuwait to Nghi Son Refinery. Negotiations were continuing, and the arrangement still required PetroVietnam approval. A VLCC would be a natural vessel for the route, but VLCC acquisition prices were high. PVTrans therefore might begin with an Aframax-based solution rather than immediately purchase a VLCC. No binding contract, start date, volume, or vessel commitment was announced.

## 30:40-35:55 — Q&A: vessel deployment, contract renewals, and 2H guidance

PVT Apollo had been moved from international employment to domestic service to replace PVT Hera while Hera was in dry dock. Once Hera returned to BSR service, Apollo would return to the international market.

Most time-charter contracts were renewed in June, leaving only a few for renewal through year-end. Domestic time-charter rates were stable. Oil and chemical tanker renewals increased by roughly USD1,000-2,000 per day. Some dry-bulk vessels remained exposed to the spot market.

Management expected PVTrans to meet both its revenue and profit plans for 2026 and believed profit would likely exceed plan. It was less certain about whether reported revenue would land exactly on the target because trading activity and rate normalization could change the outcome. Freight rates had fallen from their April-May peaks but remained healthy.

For crude tankers, management noted that domestic employment was less profitable than international employment during the April-May rate spike, but the comparison later reversed as international rates eased. By the meeting date, domestic crude rates were around USD46,000 per day versus approximately USD40,000 for international Aframax employment.

Crude transport should remain PVTrans's largest earnings contributor, but a significant increase from the 1H run rate was unlikely.
