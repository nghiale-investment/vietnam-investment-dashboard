# Executive Summary

Vinamilk presented H1 2026 as a broad-based recovery rather than a low-base rebound confined to one line item. Management reported net revenue of VND34,996bn, up 17.8% year on year; profit after tax of VND5,643bn, up 38.4%; and EPS of VND2,420, up 40.7%. Domestic net revenue reached VND14,516bn in Q2, up 6.5%, and VND26,596bn in H1, up 12.4%. Management emphasized that domestic growth was led mainly by volume, supported by a better product mix, and said the comparison remained double-digit even against 2024 rather than only the weak Q1 2025 base.

The domestic recovery was attributed to renewed growth in core categories, distribution repair, and premium innovation. Traditional trade had stopped declining and returned to growth, while modern trade and direct-to-consumer channels grew much faster. Management described the current domestic channel mix as approximately 65% traditional trade, 19% modern trade, and 16% D2C, the latter comprising Vinamilk's own stores and e-commerce. The company had about 950 own stores and targeted roughly 1,000 by year-end. Green Farm grew at a triple-digit rate, while the newly launched Optimum A2 Pro Plus 110ml ready-to-drink SKU scaled rapidly enough to become meaningful within the Optimum portfolio; additional 180ml and powder formats were being rolled out. Mộc Châu Creamery also began gaining traction after extending from its northern base into southern supermarkets and Vinamilk stores.

International operations were the other major growth engine. Management reported overseas revenue of VND4,332bn in Q2, up 39.2%, and VND8,400bn in H1, up 39.1%. Direct exports reached VND2,132bn in Q2 and VND4,056bn in H1, increasing 13.2% and 16.2%, respectively. Existing export markets remained the main absolute contributor, while new markets grew at double-digit rates from a small base. Vinamilk had reached more than 60 countries and had recently begun small shipments to Russia. Management said Middle East shipments continued during the period of greatest disruption by changing routes and commercial terms, and Q3 export volume targets remained unchanged, subject to geopolitical developments.

Angkor Milk's Cambodia business more than doubled from a low base in H1, with profit also growing at a double-digit rate. Management cautioned that the percentage growth rate would normalize in H2 because the comparison base had improved by late 2025, but said Q3-Q4 absolute revenue could remain around the Q2 level. Driftwood Dairy's profit improvement was driven more by margin than volume: better input costs, freight, packaging, and cost control supported earnings while revenue grew only modestly.

Gross margin reached 43.5% in Q2, up 170bps, and 43.1% in H1, up 200bps. Management attributed the expansion to favorable procurement, volume-led fixed-cost absorption, product-mix improvement, and operating discipline. Raw-material prices had been locked in through November for most inputs and through year-end for some. Later purchases were somewhat more expensive but remained manageable. Management expected the H2 gross margin to be broadly maintained, with any movement unlikely to be large, while continuing to launch higher-value products through H2 2026 and H1 2027.

Advertising, promotion, and display expenditure reached approximately VND3,131bn in Q2, equal to about 16.6% of net revenue. Management framed this as selective investment in brand renewal, product launches, and Vinamilk's 50th anniversary rather than uncontrolled spending. The anniversary drove particularly strong consumer promotions, but management said the full-year plan already incorporated the expenditure and should not produce a late reporting surprise. Operating and selling expenses as a percentage of revenue nevertheless edged lower, indicating operating leverage.

Capital allocation policy remained conservative and shareholder-oriented. Management explained that short-term borrowing was used for specific timing needs such as a roughly VND3.8tn dividend payment, not to fund deposits. The AGM policy remained a dividend of at least 50% of profit after tax, and management indicated an intention to preserve 2026 shareholder benefits around the 2025 level, with more detail expected in Q3 or Q4. No share-buyback plan was under consideration. Foreign ownership had fallen to around 49%, but management attributed this mainly to broad foreign outflows and emphasized operating performance rather than direct intervention in the share price.

Manufacturing assets were already highly automated from processing through filling, packing, and palletizing. The next step was digitization across 13 domestic and two overseas plants, including MES, productivity, and energy monitoring, with gradual application of AI. Annual capital expenditure was described as typically ranging around VND2.5-3.0tn, depending on project disbursement, versus as much as roughly VND3.5tn in some years. Expansion included a second 8,000-cow Tây Ninh farm on a 678-hectare site, a new plant in the same area, expansion at Bình Định, and herd growth at existing farms including Thống Nhất, Thanh Hóa. Management said domestic fresh-milk supply was sufficient for planned fresh-milk production through at least 2027-2028.

Vinamilk also presented decarbonization as an operating strategy. Management referred to SBTi-aligned targets through 2035 for FLAG and non-FLAG activities and net zero by 2050, broader use of renewable energy, ongoing energy-efficiency programs, lower-emission procurement criteria, and carbon sinks including 25 hectares of mangroves in Cà Mau and a planned 16,000 long-lived trees at Cúc Phương National Park. # Key Takeaways

1. **Domestic growth quality improved.** H1 domestic revenue rose 12.4%, driven primarily by double-digit volume growth and a richer mix rather than price increases.
2. **Premiumization is becoming financially meaningful.** Green Farm grew triple digits; Optimum A2 Pro Plus scaled quickly; premium products moved from near 10% of sales in 2025 to above 10% in 2026, although management did not disclose a precise share.
3. **Distribution repair is working.** Traditional trade returned to growth, while MT and D2C materially outperformed. The own-store network was approaching 1,000 locations.
4. **International growth is broad but should normalize.** Overseas revenue rose about 39% in H1. Angkor Milk's exceptional percentage growth will slow, though management expects the absolute run rate to remain solid.
5. **Margin improvement was not solely commodity-driven.** Procurement helped, but volume leverage, product mix, and operating discipline were also important. Management expected no major H2 gross-margin deterioration.
6. **Marketing spend is elevated by design.** Q2 advertising and promotion was high, partly because of the 50th anniversary, but was included in the annual plan and accompanied by lower operating-cost ratios.
7. **Dividend continuity remains the priority.** The payout floor remains at least 50% of profit after tax, with an intention to keep shareholder benefits near the 2025 level; buybacks are not planned.
8. **Fresh-milk capacity is being expanded ahead of demand.** New farm capacity and existing-herd expansion are intended to secure supply through at least 2028.
9. **Product innovation is capability-led.** Management highlighted ultra-filtration, high-protein formulations, A2 milk from A2 herds, and award-winning gelato rather than simple packaging or flavor extensions.
