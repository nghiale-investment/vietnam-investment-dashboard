# Executive Summary

PV Drilling described a structurally tight jack-up market and a fleet-expansion strategy built around acquiring and reactivating secondhand rigs rather than ordering newbuilds. Management cited roughly 490 jack-ups globally, of which about 430 were marketed, and global marketed utilization around 88%. The Middle East, Far East, and Southeast Asia were all tight: approximately 91% utilization in the Middle East, 100% in the Far East, and 89% in Southeast Asia. Vietnam was described as the largest source of Southeast Asian jack-up demand in 2026, requiring roughly 13 rigs out of regional demand of about 39.

Premium jack-up day rates were broadly stable at approximately USD92,000-120,000 per day globally and USD92,000-116,000 in Southeast Asia. PVD's Q2 owned-rig average was approximately USD91,000-92,000 per day, slightly below roughly USD93,000-94,000 a year earlier. PVD VI earned more than USD100,000 per day while PVD I was lower. Management saw upside when contracts renew if crude prices remain high and regional demand stays strong, but would not give a formal 2027 rate forecast.

Fleet utilization remained the core operating strength. PVD I, II, and III were at 100% utilization in H1, while most other owned rigs were around 92-94%. PVD IX showed only about 46% utilization because it entered service during the period. Operating efficiency for owned rigs was generally above 98%. PVD II and III were working for Pertamina in Indonesia for the full year; PVD VI was working in Malaysia; PVD I was on a Vietnam campaign; PVD V, the tender-assist drilling rig, was working in Brunei; and PVD VIII and IX were being prepared for Malaysian work after Vietnam campaigns and upgrades.

PVD's near-term earnings story is dominated by the commissioning and deployment of PVD VIII, IX, and X. Management had acquired PVD X in the prior month and expected delivery in October 2026, followed by reactivation and operation around April 2027. The company indicated a potential market day rate around USD92,000-95,000, but no contract had been finalized or disclosed. PVD VIII had completed a Vietnam campaign and was being upgraded before a Malaysian contract. PVD IX had been reactivated and used on an initial Vietnam campaign, but a subsequent shipyard and upgrade program proved more complex than expected, extending from an expected 60 days to at least 70 days. The lump-sum arrangement meant the company could not automatically bill more for the overrun; additional labor, equipment, and downtime costs affected Q2 and would continue into Q3. Management called this a meaningful profit headwind.

The fleet-investment economics strongly favored secondhand rigs. Management estimated a newbuild would cost roughly USD200-250mn and take about three years, with shipyard execution risk. By comparison, PVD VIII was purchased for about USD45mn and required total investment of roughly USD75mn after reactivation, taking around ten months to enter operation. Customers generally award contracts based on technical compliance and price rather than simply preferring the newest rig. Management therefore saw better risk-adjusted returns from suitable secondhand assets and stated that PVD's 2027-2030 strategy included two or three additional jack-ups beyond PVD X, although purchases may not begin in 2027 and will depend on project timing, prices, specifications, and reuse of existing spares.

PVD continued to favor overseas contracts because Malaysian and Indonesian campaigns are longer and easier to sequence than fragmented Vietnam programs. Management said Malaysian rates were somewhat higher than domestic rates. PVD VIII and IX had signed H2 overseas contracts, although rates were confidential. PVD wanted to add a third rig in Indonesia in 2027 and expand well-technical services through its Indonesian joint venture. Domestically, where campaigns are often short and separated by idle periods, PVD may charter rigs from partners rather than permanently redeploy owned units. This approach preserves domestic market share while protecting fleet utilization.

Management reported Q2 revenue of approximately VND3tn, profit before tax of about VND206.1bn, and profit after tax of about VND178bn. H1 completed around 58% of the revenue plan, 61% of the PBT plan, and 60% of the PAT plan. Versus H1 2025, revenue increased approximately 51%, PBT 27%, and PAT 22%. Drilling services contributed about 65% of revenue, with well-technical and other services making up the balance. The exact service split beyond drilling was not fully clear in the recording.

Q2 profit growth lagged revenue because the comparison period included an insurance-related gain associated with PVD VI, while 2026 had higher input and freight costs, increased interest expense from financing PVD VIII-IX-X, readiness costs for PVD X before delivery, and the PVD IX shipyard overrun. Management cited more than VND100bn of incremental financial cost, but the exact number requires confirmation. The Q2 effective tax rate above 20% was attributed generally to overseas taxation; IR deferred a detailed answer pending review of the financial statements and promised to follow up by email.

Well-related services had an unusually strong 2025 and could not repeat that growth automatically without further investment in equipment, tools, specialized manpower, and overseas expansion. Management highlighted PVD Logging, PVD Well Services, and the manpower/service businesses as positioned for good 2026 performance, but not another step-change comparable with the prior year.

For PVD V, the tender-assist rig in Brunei, the current contract was signed during a weak market and is now below prevailing rates. The transcript appears to describe a six-year firm period plus a four-year option beginning in January 2022. Management expected renewal discussions around mid-2027 and would seek a higher rate. Returning the rig to Vietnam is an alternative only if a deepwater campaign is long enough—roughly three to five years—to cover mobilization and protect economics. Shorter work would more likely be served with a partner's chartered unit.

Block B–Ô Môn remained strategically important but execution timing was uncertain. Management said Phase 1A was already drilling with a partner rig, while PVD had a Phase 1B commitment. Technical delays shifted the expected start into a broad March-August 2027 window. PVD would not leave PVD VI idle for an uncertain five-month window and instead planned to extend its Malaysian employment, then potentially serve Block B with another, likely chartered, rig. Management's priority was continuous utilization over reserving an owned rig for a short, uncertain domestic campaign.

Management did not expect Middle East rig suspensions to flood Southeast Asia. Middle East utilization remained higher than Southeast Asia, suspended units were returning, and Saudi Aramco was again seeking rigs. Older cold- or warm-stacked rigs also face technical barriers. Increasingly common tender requirements for offline activity/stand-building capability reduce the competitiveness of older units. PVD IX and VI had been upgraded, while PVD I could not economically support the feature and therefore remained suited to less-demanding domestic tenders.

# Key Takeaways

1. **The regional jack-up market remains tight.** Southeast Asian marketed utilization was about 89%, with Vietnam representing the largest 2026 demand pool in the region.
2. **PVD's owned fleet is highly utilized.** Most rigs operated at 92-100% utilization and above 98% efficiency; PVD IX was the exception because it only began operating during H1.
3. **Day rates are stable, not yet accelerating.** Q2 average owned-rig rates were around USD91,000-92,000/day, slightly below the prior year, but management sees renewal upside if the oil and rig markets remain firm.
4. **PVD IX is the main near-term cost risk.** A reactivation/upgrade program exceeded its planned duration under a lump-sum structure, raising Q2 and Q3 costs and delaying revenue.
5. **PVD X is the next earnings catalyst.** Delivery was expected in October 2026 and operation around April 2027, with a preliminary market-rate expectation near USD92,000-95,000/day.
6. **Secondhand acquisition economics remain compelling.** PVD compared about USD75mn total investment and ten months for PVD VIII with roughly USD200-250mn and three years for a newbuild.
7. **Overseas continuity takes priority over domestic presence.** Longer Malaysian and Indonesian programs support utilization; chartered partner rigs can cover short Vietnam campaigns.
8. **Two or three more jack-ups are possible after PVD X.** Timing depends on rig prices, specifications, project visibility, and demand; management did not commit to a 2027 purchase.
9. **Q2 profit quality was affected by identifiable items.** Higher finance and readiness costs, input inflation, PVD IX overruns, and the absence of a prior-year insurance gain weighed on earnings.
10. **Block B–Ô Môn is a catalyst with timing risk.** Start uncertainty makes it uneconomic to reserve PVD VI, so PVD may use another or chartered rig when the campaign becomes firm.

## 00:03-00:08 - Oil-price and global jack-up outlook

Management identified Middle East geopolitical tension as the principal macro factor affecting oil and drilling markets in H1. Brent had moved sharply during the year and remained high enough to support upstream work. PVD cited roughly 490 jack-ups globally, with about 430 marketed and marketed utilization near 88%. Around 30% of the fleet was more than 35 years old, making maintenance, technical compliance, and contract awards increasingly difficult for older rigs. Only a limited number of newbuilds were expected during 2027-2029.

Premium jack-up day rates were around USD92,000-120,000 per day, with newer high-specification rigs sometimes reaching approximately USD130,000. Demand and rates were broadly stable versus Q1.

## 00:08-00:14 - Middle East, Far East, and Southeast Asian utilization

The Middle East had approximately 91% utilization as suspended rigs returned and Saudi Aramco sought additional units. The Far East had roughly 63 rigs and effectively 100% utilization, reducing the risk that Chinese, Korean, or Japanese units would move into Southeast Asia. Southeast Asia had about 39 rigs of demand in 2026 and marketed utilization near 89%.

PVD argued that jack-ups are the most versatile rig type in Asia-Pacific shallow-water markets and can move among Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, and other regional markets. Rates in Southeast Asia were roughly USD92,000-116,000 for premium units. Vietnam had the largest regional demand at approximately 13 rigs. PVD's six delivered jack-ups were all working; PVD X had not yet been received.

PVD VIII had completed a Vietnam campaign and returned to the shipyard for upgrades before Malaysia.
