# Executive Summary

Coteccons framed FY2026 as the transition point from scale growth to quality growth. Management reported record revenue of approximately VND34.4tn, up 38% year on year and about 15% above plan. Profit was described at roughly VND788bn, up 73% and equivalent to approximately 113% of the annual target. Gross margin expanded to around 4.2%, nearly one percentage point above the prior year. Backlog doubled from roughly VND35tn to more than VND70tn, providing two to three years of revenue visibility. Repeat sales were cited at approximately 72-75%, indicating that existing clients account for most new work.

The strategic message was explicit: revenue is no longer the primary target. CTD intends to select better clients, technically complex projects, and contracts with sound margins; improve gross and net margin; raise ROE; and protect execution quality. Management reiterated a long-term ambition of 20-30% profit growth over the next five years, supported by backlog conversion, infrastructure, FDI/industrial projects, overseas work, and operating efficiency. It did not give a precise FY2027 revenue, profit, or margin target, saying formal guidance would follow at the AGM.

Infrastructure is becoming a second growth engine alongside civil construction. CTD highlighted a major Cần Giờ-related bridge/infrastructure project, Gia Bình International Airport, infrastructure and facilities in Phú Quốc, Long Thành Airport packages, and other national projects. The Gia Bình air-traffic-control-related structure had topped out after approximately six months, much faster than a cited comparison project. Management acknowledged labor and material constraints but said the highlighted projects remained on schedule. CTD described its infrastructure proposition through the “FAST” concept: financial capability; assurance on safety, quality, and sustainability; speed; and technology/innovation.

The backlog mix is increasingly diversified. CTD cited international/FDI clients requiring advanced safety and sustainability standards, including a GE high-voltage direct-current-related factory in Hải Phòng, alongside residential and commercial projects from Vingroup, Masterise, Sun Group, and Nam Long. Infrastructure projects may carry attractive economics because scale and technical complexity create barriers to entry, although management would not disclose segment margins or standalone contract values. It said approximately 70-80% of new contracts are negotiated/repeat-sales assignments from strategic clients rather than open tenders; open competition is more common for FDI work.

Margin improvement was presented as operational rather than one-off. Q4 gross margin was discussed at around 4.5%, while the full-year figure was approximately 4.2%. Management said there were no abnormal Q4 gross-profit bookings. The improvement came from client and project selection, centralized procurement, supplier diversification, framework agreements that lock critical-material prices for periods such as six to twelve months, advance payments to vendors, value engineering, AI and technology, site-cost control, better equipment utilization, and stronger governance. Strategic clients also shared some input-cost increases through negotiation. Management wants further margin improvement in FY2027 but did not endorse a specific 5%, 6%, or 7% target.

Working capital improved materially despite the revenue step-up. Management cited operating cash flow moving from approximately negative VND1.2tn in the prior year to positive more than VND0.8tn, a roughly VND2tn improvement. Cash reportedly increased from around VND4tn to VND8tn. Receivables stayed near VND15tn even as revenue rose from about VND25tn to VND34-35tn. Customer advances increased from roughly VND4.8tn to VND9tn. Management nevertheless agreed that receivables, advances, payables, and cash conversion remain central financial priorities.

The VND526bn Tân Hoàng Minh legacy receivable was the most important balance-sheet discussion. CTD has fully provisioned the exposure, so no additional loss is expected from the existing amount. After years of negotiation, management said it had reached positive high-level progress and a preliminary agreement framework that could include a broader long-term partnership. Under Vietnamese accounting rules, provision reversals would be recorded only when real cash is collected. Recovery is expected in phases rather than immediately; the amount and timing depend on negotiation and customer cash flow, with more detail targeted for the AGM.

Other provisions were also described as legacy-project exposures rather than new-project problems. CTD booked around VND70bn of provision in Q4 after several quarters without new charges. Management emphasized that newer projects are screened more strictly and that it prefers to recognize potential legacy risks rather than hide them. Some further legacy provisions may arise, but management aims to offset them with reversals so the net provision impact is around zero or better. This is an aspiration, not a guarantee.

The balance sheet retained significant liquidity and generated net financial income—management cited around VND16bn despite higher interest rates—because deposits and borrowing are actively balanced. CTD continued to explore longer-tenor offshore funding to reduce dependence on short-term bank loans, but high hedging costs and market pricing made the timing unattractive. A share buyback had been discussed, but management made no firm commitment, size, approval, or timetable.

The foreign ownership limit increase was in progress through shareholder and regulatory approvals. Management expected completion within six to nine months, described as by the third quarter of FY2027—approximately calendar Q1 2027. The outcome is partly dependent on authorities and therefore outside CTD's sole control. Management declined to announce stock splits or stock dividends as liquidity measures, prioritizing business performance and disclosure.

Human resources were treated as an execution differentiator. Management acknowledged that Vingroup and other companies had recruited some junior employees but said key middle and senior managers remained. CTD emphasized training, clear career paths, transparent total rewards, university partnerships, and an “Own It” empowerment culture. For subcontracted workers, CTD highlighted safety, welfare, scholarships, and programs that reached more than 40,000 workers. At a major Phú Quốc site, more than 3,000 workers were mobilized through a holiday period. Labor scarcity had not prevented approximately 40% revenue growth because technology, planning, and project management reduced labor intensity.

Material-cost inflation was managed through both contract and procurement levers. CTD worked with strategic clients to adjust for increases, centralized purchasing to aggregate volume, negotiated framework prices with vendors, used advance payments and committed quantities to secure favorable rates, reserved material at sites, and applied forecasting and scenario planning. Management said these measures protected Q4 margin even as input prices rose.

On Long Thành Airport, management said CTD's hangar and parking-related packages continued and were approaching handover. It did not directly answer whether CTD or its packages were subjects of the broader investigation, referring investors to official government releases. CTD said the situation increased internal caution, risk review, and at times slowed decisions. This remains an unresolved governance and execution-monitoring item.

Management viewed high interest rates as an indirect risk to residential end users and developers, but argued that its clients were generally large, capable groups and that diversification toward infrastructure, industrial, and overseas work reduced dependence on residential construction. Residential still represented roughly 40% of the business based on the discussion, although the exact segment mix was not fully stated.

9. **No precise FY2027 guidance was given.** Management wants higher margins, profit, and ROE, but specific targets are reserved for the AGM.
