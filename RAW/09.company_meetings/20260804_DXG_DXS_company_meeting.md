# Executive Summary

The central message was Dat Xanh's transition from a conventional residential developer into BLUEMARQ Group, an integrated real-estate investment and real-asset-management platform. The restructuring, completed in May 2026, separates investment, development, asset management, and the Dat Xanh operating platform into four pillars. Management wants to recycle capital more actively, admit project-level partners, accelerate monetization of the land bank, and build fee-based and investment-management income alongside traditional development profit. It described a 12-project pipeline with approximately three million square metres of gross floor area for 2025-2030. The portfolio was said to be divided roughly evenly between central-business-district or prime projects and transit-oriented developments, with indicative sales potential of USD4.5 billion and USD1.8 billion respectively. These numbers should be treated as long-range portfolio potential rather than booked backlog or formal earnings guidance.

DXG also strengthened its capital base. In June, the company completed a 14% bonus-share issuance from retained equity, taking charter capital to nearly VND13 trillion, approximately 24% higher year on year. Management said the process was completed within 41 days. The enlarged capital base, project-level financing, and prospective outside investors are intended to support the next development cycle while keeping leverage controlled.

The Privé is the dominant near-term operating and earnings driver. The project comprises more than 3,000 apartments across 12 towers. Official Dat Xanh materials identify 3,175 units. Phase one achieved strong reservations in 2025, and phase two—towers 9, 10, and 11—was brought to market in 2026. Cloud9 Tower, tower 9, opened for booking in June and entered the July sales campaign with more than 60 agencies and over 1,700 sales representatives. Management said approximately half of the project's total inventory had been reserved or sold by the meeting date and maintained an ambition to sell all ordinary inventory during 2026. Jewel Tower, tower 12, is positioned as the premium product and is being held back until the surrounding towers have sold further.

The Privé's achieved pricing was stronger than initially planned. Management had expected approximately VND99-120 million per square metre but reported current levels near VND130-135 million. The average apartment ticket was around VND10 billion. Pricing for towers 9-11 was kept broadly similar despite the strong first phase because mortgage rates had risen and competing launches—including Global City and other eastern HCMC projects—were increasing customer choice. Buyer packages included low initial payments and interest support for approximately 24-30 months. DXG capped the subsidized borrowing rate at around 10% annually for the first two years and included the support cost in selling prices.

Management expected The Privé to deliver a gross margin of at least 48%. Important de-risking factors included full payment of land-use fees, repayment of the outstanding project loan to MB Bank in November 2025, and a locked phase-two construction price with China State Construction Engineering Corporation as general contractor. Towers 5-8 were targeted for handover in 2H2027. The remaining project was expected to complete through 2027-2028, with early 2029 presented as an outer case. The principal remaining risks are sales conversion, construction execution, and pricing competition rather than land-use-fee or legacy project-debt uncertainty.

DXG's Q2 headline revenue was approximately VND748 billion, down 45% quarter on quarter but up 2% year on year. NPAT attributable to parent shareholders was approximately VND53 billion, up 39% quarter on quarter and 41% year on year. Services generated 77% of revenue, comprising around 35% brokerage and 42% other services, while property development contributed 23%. Management explained that the unusually large “other services” line largely reflected stricter classification under Circular 99: building management and leasing, previously grouped with brokerage, were reclassified. It should therefore not be read as a sudden emergence of a new high-growth business.

DXG reported gross margin around 48%, helped by the service-heavy revenue mix. Inventory was approximately VND15.985 trillion, cash and equivalents approximately VND1.871 trillion, and borrowings about VND5.1 trillion. Management emphasized a diversified debt structure and disciplined leverage. Q2 property-development recognition came mainly from residual Gem Sky World shophouse handovers. Around 80 units were handed over in 1H; some cancelled sales returned to inventory. Management said the project was around 60% sold and only roughly 5% of sold units still awaited handover, with the remainder concentrated in shophouses.

DXS's Q2 revenue was approximately VND630 billion, up about 120% year on year but down 56% quarter on quarter. The central region generated around 52%, supported by project-development recognition. The transcript did not capture the project names reliably enough to assign the revenue precisely. DXS recorded an NPAT attributable to parent loss of approximately VND36.7 billion because of stricter provisions against receivables and deposits at subsidiaries, including Dat Xanh Miền Trung and Dat Xanh Miền Bắc.

Management described the provisioned deposits as advances paid to smaller developers in exchange for exclusive distribution rights. When project execution was delayed, some balances remained outstanding for one or two years and had to be provisioned under a more conservative approach. The company said the balances were supported by security or relationship evidence provided to the auditor. EY was reviewing the half-year statements, which management expected to publish by the end of August without a major difference from the announced figures. It did not expect another provision of similar scale in 2H, but recovery of the underlying deposits remains a key monitoring point.

DXS maintained a workforce of more than 3,500 and a nationwide distribution system. Management remained constructive on transaction activity in 2H as major developers returned with more supply, discounts, and buyer-support packages. Its internal research suggested the market could sustain an absorption rate of roughly 25-40% if mortgage rates remained around 11-13%. Demand in HCMC and Bình Dương was described as healthy for well-located, good-quality products; the North remained relatively resilient through Dat Xanh Miền Bắc, while the Central and Mekong regions were softer.

Management acknowledged that Q2 sentiment weakened as lending rates increased quickly and several large projects experienced launch or execution problems. Landed products were particularly sensitive. The recovery thesis therefore depends on supply quality and affordability rather than a broad market rebound. DXS also has project-development and construction income. Q2 construction-contract revenue was approximately VND170 billion from Regal Group's self-performed construction work, but management said this activity was intended to control schedule and customization and contributed little gross profit.

The next DXG development wave is expected to include three projects beginning construction in late 2026 or early 2027 and entering presales in 2027. The transcript appears to refer to Opal Cityview, DXH Parkview, and another Opal project, but the third name was not sufficiently clear to certify. Land-use-fee estimates had been received for two projects, with payment expected in Q3 or Q4. Banks had agreed in principle to fund the remaining payments and project development. Management said DXG had already funded approximately 30% of relevant land cost and could borrow the remaining 70%, with full project financing available after the legal and fee milestones.

Gem City and Gem Premium offer longer-dated upside. Gem City was admitted to the Resolution 171 pilot mechanism in April. DXG was working on investment approval and a revised 1/500 master plan. The project could benefit from planned metro and road infrastructure, but the exact road reference and proposed floor-area ratio were distorted in the recording. Gem Premium had one remaining resettlement case, with compensation already advanced. Management described approximately one kilometre of river frontage, access from Phạm Văn Đồng Boulevard and a planned river road, and a mixed-use condominium, office, and commercial concept targeted for implementation by 2030.

The investment-platform strategy is potentially important but not yet monetized. DXG was discussing both majority and minority project-level participation with foreign developers and financial investors, a change from the historical preference for full ownership. The objective is to realize some profit and cash earlier, recycle capital into the land bank, and share development risk. Management was also studying a fund structure with a global fund manager and the emerging Vietnam International Financial Centre framework. No partner, fund, transaction size, or launch date was announced, so this remains strategic optionality rather than a near-term forecast item.

Overall, The Privé provides the clearest near-term catalyst and margin visibility. The next project pipeline, Gem City and Gem Premium legal progress, and investment-platform partnerships offer additional upside but require specific approvals and capital commitments. DXS could benefit from higher 2H transaction activity, but the Q2 provision demonstrates the balance-sheet risk embedded in deposits with smaller developers. Higher mortgage rates and increased competing supply remain the common constraint across both DXG development and DXS distribution.

## 55:25-60:42 — Q&A: DXS market outlook, regional conditions, and project handovers

DXS expected more transactions in 2H as major developers launched additional supply with discounts and financing support. Its research suggested a 25-40% absorption range if lending rates remained around 11-13%. Management acknowledged that Q2 activity paused because rates rose quickly and several large launches experienced problems, weakening sentiment, particularly for landed products.

Demand in HCMC and Bình Dương remained healthy for projects with good locations, legal status, and product quality. Dat Xanh Miền Bắc continued to perform relatively well in the North, while the Central and Mekong regions were softer.

DXS also discussed ready or upcoming handover inventory in its Regal-related portfolio, including completed shophouses in Đà Nẵng and apartments at Regal Complex. The transcript's unit count conflicted with official project information, so the clean record does not assign a definitive remaining inventory figure. Management closed by reiterating its expectation of a stronger 2H transaction environment, while recognizing that mortgage costs and project execution would determine actual recovery.
